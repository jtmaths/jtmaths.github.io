1 The Big Idea

Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty. Statistics looks at data that have already happened; probability builds models of what could happen. The two meet in hypothesis testing, where a probability model is used to judge whether real data are surprising.

Year 1 has two halves. First, the language and rules for combining events — Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, mutually exclusive and independent events. Second, random variables: a number decided by chance, described by a probability distribution. The most important one at AS is the binomial distribution, which counts successes in a fixed number of independent trials.

3 The Five Core Techniques
1 — The addition rule
$\mathrm{P}(A \cup B) = \mathrm{P}(A) + \mathrm{P}(B) - \mathrm{P}(A \cap B)$
2 — Testing independence
Compare $\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)$ with $\mathrm{P}(A) \times \mathrm{P}(B)$
3 — Tree diagrams
Multiply along branches; add the paths you want
4 — A probability distribution
$\sum \mathrm{P}(X = x) = 1$ — use this to find unknown constants
5 — The binomial distribution
$\mathrm{P}(X = r) = \dbinom{n}{r} p^r (1-p)^{n-r}$;   $\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant r) = 1 - \mathrm{P}(X \leqslant r - 1)$
Worked Example — Several techniques in one question

At a café, each customer independently pays by card with probability $0.3$. Of the next $8$ customers, let $X$ be the number who pay by card. Find (a) $\mathrm{P}(X = 2)$, (b) $\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant 3)$, (c) the probability that the first two customers both pay by card.

①Fixed $n = 8$, two outcomes, independent, constant $p$: $X \sim \mathrm{B}(8, 0.3)$.
②(a) $\mathrm{P}(X = 2) = \dbinom82 (0.3)^2 (0.7)^6 = 28 \times 0.09 \times 0.117649 = 0.2965$.
③(b) $\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant 3) = 1 - \mathrm{P}(X \leqslant 2) = 1 - 0.5518 = 0.4482$.
④(c) Independent events: $0.3 \times 0.3 = 0.09$.

5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
Confusing mutually exclusive with independent. Mutually exclusive means no overlap; independent means $\mathrm{P}(A \cap B) = \mathrm{P}(A)\mathrm{P}(B)$. Events with non-zero probabilities cannot be both.
Forgetting to subtract the overlap. $\mathrm{P}(A \cup B) = \mathrm{P}(A) + \mathrm{P}(B)$ only when $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive.
Missing the $\binom{n}{r}$. $(0.3)^2(0.7)^6$ is the probability of one particular order. There are $\binom82 = 28$ orders.
Off-by-one with inequalities. $\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant 3) = 1 - \mathrm{P}(X \leqslant 2)$, not $1 - \mathrm{P}(X \leqslant 3)$.
Generic assumptions. "The trials are independent" earns little; "whether one seed germinates does not affect whether another does" earns the mark.

7 Practice Questions

Ten questions drawing on the whole Probability topic. Work each one through before opening the solution.

Question 1

$A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive with $\mathrm{P}(A) = 0.3$ and $\mathrm{P}(B) = 0.5$. Find $\mathrm{P}(A \cup B)$.

▶ Show solution

$0.3 + 0.5 = 0.8$

Question 2

$A$ and $B$ are independent with $\mathrm{P}(A) = 0.4$ and $\mathrm{P}(B) = 0.5$. Find $\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)$ and $\mathrm{P}(A \cup B)$.

▶ Show solution

$\mathrm{P}(A \cap B) = 0.2$;   $\mathrm{P}(A \cup B) = 0.4 + 0.5 - 0.2 = 0.7$.

Question 3

Of $30$ people, $12$ own a cat, $15$ own a dog and $5$ own both. Find the probability that a person chosen at random owns (a) a cat but not a dog, (b) neither.

▶ Show solution

Cat only $7$, dog only $10$, both $5$, so neither $= 30 - 22 = 8$.

(a) $\dfrac{7}{30}$   (b) $\dfrac{8}{30} = \dfrac{4}{15}$

Question 4

A bag has $3$ red and $2$ blue counters. A counter is taken, replaced, and a second is taken. Find the probability of one of each colour.

▶ Show solution

RB or BR: $2 \times \tfrac35 \times \tfrac25 = \dfrac{12}{25}$.

Question 5

$\mathrm{P}(X = x) = \dfrac{x}{c}$ for $x = 1, 2, 3, 4$. Find $c$ and $\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant 3)$.

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{1 + 2 + 3 + 4}{c} = 1$, so $c = 10$.

$\mathrm{P}(X \geqslant 3) = \dfrac{3 + 4}{10} = 0.7$.

Question 6

A fair coin is tossed $6$ times. Find the probability of exactly $3$ heads.

▶ Show solution

$X \sim \mathrm{B}(6, 0.5)$: $\dbinom63 (0.5)^6 = \dfrac{20}{64} = 0.3125$.

Question 7

$X \sim \mathrm{B}(10, 0.3)$. Find $\mathrm{P}(X > 2)$.

▶ Show solution

$1 - \mathrm{P}(X \leqslant 2) = 1 - 0.3828 = 0.6172$

Question 8

$10\%$ of a population is left-handed. In a random sample of $25$, find the probability that (a) at most $2$, (b) more than $3$ are left-handed.

▶ Show solution

$L \sim \mathrm{B}(25, 0.1)$.

(a) $\mathrm{P}(L \leqslant 2) = 0.5371$

(b) $\mathrm{P}(L > 3) = 1 - \mathrm{P}(L \leqslant 3) = 1 - 0.7636 = 0.2364$

Question 9

Explain whether $\mathrm{B}(n, p)$ is suitable for: (a) the number of sixes in $20$ rolls of a fair die; (b) the number of days it rains in a week in March.

▶ Show solution

(a) Yes: $n = 20$ fixed, six or not six, rolls independent, $p = \tfrac16$ constant. $\mathrm{B}\left(20, \tfrac16\right)$.

(b) Doubtful: weather on consecutive days is not independent (wet spells last several days), and the probability of rain may vary through the week.

Question 10

A machine produces items of which $5\%$ are defective, independently. Items are packed in boxes of $20$.

(a) Find the probability that a box contains no defective items.   (b) Find the probability that a box contains at least $2$ defective items.   (c) A shop receives $10$ boxes. Find the probability that none of them contains at least $2$ defective items.   (d) Give one reason why the binomial model might be unrealistic.

▶ Show solution

$D \sim \mathrm{B}(20, 0.05)$.

(a) $\mathrm{P}(D = 0) = 0.95^{20} = 0.3585$

(b) $\mathrm{P}(D = 1) = 20(0.05)(0.95)^{19} = 0.3774$, so $\mathrm{P}(D \leqslant 1) = 0.7358$ and $\mathrm{P}(D \geqslant 2) = 0.2642$.

(c) Each box independently has probability $0.7358$ of fewer than $2$ defectives: $0.73584^{10} = 0.0465$.

(d) Faults may come in runs — for example if the machine goes out of adjustment, several consecutive items will be defective — so items are not independent and $p$ may not be constant.

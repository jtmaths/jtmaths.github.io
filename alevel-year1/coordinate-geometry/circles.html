1 The Equation of a Circle

A circle is the set of all points at a fixed distance $r$ (the radius) from a fixed point (the centre). Writing that sentence with the distance formula gives the equation. This page completes DfE content area C · Coordinate geometry.

Circle with centre $(a, b)$ and radius $r$
$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$
Where it comes from. A point $(x, y)$ is on the circle exactly when its distance from $(a, b)$ is $r$: $\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2} = r$. Squaring both sides gives the formula. With centre the origin it becomes $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$.
Signs inside the brackets are opposite to the centre. $(x + 4)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 9$ has centre $(-4, 1)$, and radius $3$ — not $9$.
Worked Example 1 — Writing down the equation

Find the equation of the circle with centre $(3, -2)$ and radius $5$, and show that $(6, 2)$ lies on it.

①$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$
②At $(6, 2)$: $(6 - 3)^2 + (2 + 2)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$ ✓
Inside, on or outside? Substitute the point into $(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2$ and compare with $r^2$: less means inside, equal means on, greater means outside.
2 Finding the Centre and Radius by Completing the Square

If you expand $(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25$ you get $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$, which hides the centre and radius. To get them back, complete the square in $x$ and in $y$ separately.

  1. Group the $x$ terms and the $y$ terms. (If $x^2$ and $y^2$ have a common coefficient other than $1$, divide through first.)
  2. Complete the square on each: $x^2 + px \equiv \left(x + \tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2$.
  3. Move all the constants to the right-hand side. That number is $r^2$.
Worked Example 2 — Centre and radius

Find the centre and radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$.

①$x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9$  and  $y^2 + 4y = (y + 2)^2 - 4$
②$(x-3)^2 - 9 + (y+2)^2 - 4 - 12 = 0$
③$(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25$
④Centre $(3, -2)$, radius $5$.
Worked Example 3 — Divide first, and when is it a circle?

(a) Find the centre and radius of $2x^2 + 2y^2 + 8x - 12y + 8 = 0$.   (b) For what values of $k$ does $x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y + k = 0$ represent a circle?

①(a) Divide by $2$: $x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 6y + 4 = 0$
②$(x+2)^2 - 4 + (y-3)^2 - 9 + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x+2)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 9$
③Centre $(-2, 3)$, radius $3$.
④(b) $(x+1)^2 - 1 + (y-2)^2 - 4 + k = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x+1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 5 - k$
⑤A circle needs $r^2 > 0$, so $5 - k > 0$, i.e. $k < 5$.
Only if the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal (and there is no $xy$ term) can the equation be a circle. $x^2 + 2y^2 = 9$ is not a circle.
3 Three Circle Theorems in Coordinates

The subject content names three properties from GCSE geometry that you must be able to use with coordinates.

PropertyHow it is used
The angle in a semicircle is a right angleIf $AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is on the circle, then $AC \perp BC$. Conversely, if $\angle ACB = 90°$, then $AB$ is a diameter.
The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chordThe perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre.
The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contactTangent gradient $= -\dfrac{1}{\text{radius gradient}}$.
Worked Example 4 — The tangent at a point

Find the equation of the tangent to $(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25$ at $P(6, 2)$.

①Gradient of the radius $CP$: $\dfrac{2 - (-2)}{6 - 3} = \dfrac43$
②The tangent is perpendicular to the radius: gradient $-\tfrac34$.
③$y - 2 = -\tfrac34(x - 6) \;\Rightarrow\; 4y - 8 = -3x + 18$
④$3x + 4y - 26 = 0$ (the red line in the diagram above).
Worked Example 5 — Angle in a semicircle

$A(-1, 2)$ and $B(5, 10)$ are the ends of a diameter. Find the equation of the circle, and show that $\angle ACB = 90°$ where $C(6, 9)$.

①Centre = midpoint of $AB$ = $(2, 6)$.
②$r^2 = (5 - 2)^2 + (10 - 6)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$, so $(x-2)^2 + (y-6)^2 = 25$.
③$C$: $(6-2)^2 + (9-6)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$ ✓, so $C$ is on the circle.
④$m_{AC} = \dfrac{9 - 2}{6 + 1} = 1$,  $m_{BC} = \dfrac{9 - 10}{6 - 5} = -1$. Product $-1$, so $\angle ACB = 90°$ ✓
Worked Example 6 — The circle through three points

Find the equation of the circle through $A(0, 5)$, $B(7, 4)$ and $C(8, 1)$.

①Chord $AB$: midpoint $(3.5, 4.5)$, gradient $-\tfrac17$, so perpendicular gradient $7$. Bisector: $y - 4.5 = 7(x - 3.5)$, i.e. $y = 7x - 20$.
②Chord $BC$: midpoint $(7.5, 2.5)$, gradient $\dfrac{1 - 4}{8 - 7} = -3$, so perpendicular gradient $\tfrac13$. Bisector: $y - 2.5 = \tfrac13(x - 7.5)$, i.e. $y = \tfrac13 x$.
③Both bisectors pass through the centre: $7x - 20 = \tfrac13 x \;\Rightarrow\; 21x - 60 = x \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3$, $y = 1$.
④$r^2 = (0 - 3)^2 + (5 - 1)^2 = 25$, so $(x-3)^2 + (y-1)^2 = 25$.
⑤Check $C$: $25 + 0 = 25$ ✓
4 Where a Line Meets a Circle

Substitute the line into the circle. You get a quadratic, and its discriminant tells you how many points of intersection there are.

Discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$Number of intersectionsGeometry
$> 0$TwoThe line is a chord (a secant)
$= 0$One (repeated root)The line is a tangent
$< 0$NoneThe line misses the circle
Worked Example 7 — Points of intersection

Find where the line $y = x + 1$ meets the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

①$x^2 + (x+1)^2 = 25 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + x - 12 = 0$
②$(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 3$ or $x = -4$.
③Points: $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$.
Worked Example 8 — A tangency condition

Find the values of $k$ for which $y = x + k$ is a tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 8$.

①$x^2 + (x + k)^2 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 + 2kx + k^2 - 8 = 0$
②Tangent $\iff$ discriminant $= 0$: $(2k)^2 - 4(2)(k^2 - 8) = 0$
③$4k^2 - 8k^2 + 64 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; k^2 = 16$
④$k = 4$ or $k = -4$ — one tangent on each side of the circle.
Worked Example 9 — A chord and its midpoint

The line $y = 1$ cuts the circle $(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25$ at $P$ and $Q$. Find the length $PQ$ and verify that the perpendicular from the centre bisects $PQ$.

①$(x - 3)^2 + 9 = 25 \;\Rightarrow\; (x-3)^2 = 16 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 7$ or $x = -1$.
②$P(-1, 1)$, $Q(7, 1)$, so $PQ = 8$.
③The perpendicular from $C(3, -2)$ to the horizontal chord is the vertical line $x = 3$, meeting it at $(3, 1)$ — the midpoint of $PQ$ ✓
"Show detailed reasoning" questions on intersections expect the substitution, the quadratic and the discriminant written out. A calculator's equation solver is fine for checking, but not as the method.
5 Quick Reference

Standard form

$(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2$

Centre signs

Opposite to the signs in the brackets.

Expanded form

Complete the square in $x$ and $y$; the right side is $r^2$.

Is it a circle?

Need $r^2 > 0$.

Semicircle

Diameter $AB$, $C$ on circle $\Rightarrow AC \perp BC$.

Chord

Its perpendicular bisector passes through the centre.

Tangent

Perpendicular to the radius at the contact point.

Line meets circle

Substitute; the discriminant counts the intersections.

Tangent condition

$b^2 - 4ac = 0$.

Point test

Compare $(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2$ with $r^2$.

6 Practice Questions
Question 1

Write down the centre and radius of $(x + 4)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 49$.

▶ Show solution

Centre $(-4, 1)$, radius $\sqrt{49} = 7$.

Question 2

Find the equation of the circle with centre $(-1, 5)$ that passes through $(2, 1)$.

▶ Show solution

$r^2 = (2 + 1)^2 + (1 - 5)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$

$(x + 1)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 25$

Question 3

Find the centre and radius of $x^2 + y^2 + 10x - 8y + 5 = 0$.

▶ Show solution

$(x + 5)^2 - 25 + (y - 4)^2 - 16 + 5 = 0$

$(x+5)^2 + (y-4)^2 = 36$

Centre $(-5, 4)$, radius $6$.

Question 4

$(-3, 4)$ and $(5, -2)$ are the ends of a diameter of a circle. Find its equation.

▶ Show solution

Centre = midpoint $= (1, 1)$.

$r^2 = (5 - 1)^2 + (-2 - 1)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$

$(x - 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 25$

Question 5

Show that $(5, 7)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 6y - 12 = 0$ and find the equation of the tangent there in the form $ax + by + c = 0$.

▶ Show solution

$25 + 49 - 20 - 42 - 12 = 0$ ✓, so $(5, 7)$ is on the circle.

Completing the square: $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 25$, centre $(2, 3)$.

Radius gradient $\dfrac{7 - 3}{5 - 2} = \dfrac43$, so tangent gradient $-\tfrac34$.

$y - 7 = -\tfrac34(x - 5) \;\Rightarrow\; 4y - 28 = -3x + 15$

$3x + 4y - 43 = 0$

Question 6

Find the coordinates of the points where $y = x + 2$ meets $x^2 + y^2 = 10$.

▶ Show solution

$x^2 + (x + 2)^2 = 10 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 + 4x - 6 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + 2x - 3 = 0$

$(x + 3)(x - 1) = 0$, so $x = 1$ or $x = -3$.

Points $(1, 3)$ and $(-3, -1)$.

Question 7

In this question you must show detailed reasoning. Show that the line $x + y = 10$ does not meet the circle $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 4$.

▶ Show solution

Substitute $y = 10 - x$: $(x - 2)^2 + (9 - x)^2 = 4$

$x^2 - 4x + 4 + x^2 - 18x + 81 - 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 - 22x + 81 = 0$

Discriminant: $(-22)^2 - 4(2)(81) = 484 - 648 = -164 < 0$

No real roots, so the line and circle do not meet.

Question 8

The line $y = mx$ is a tangent to the circle $(x - 4)^2 + y^2 = 4$. Find the exact possible values of $m$.

▶ Show solution

$(x - 4)^2 + m^2x^2 = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; (1 + m^2)x^2 - 8x + 12 = 0$

Tangent: $64 - 4(1 + m^2)(12) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 1 + m^2 = \tfrac{64}{48} = \tfrac43$

$m^2 = \tfrac13$, so $m = \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}$.

Question 9

The equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 2y + k = 0$ represents a circle. (a) Find the range of possible values of $k$.   (b) Find $k$ if the circle touches the $x$-axis.

▶ Show solution

(a) $(x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 1)^2 - 1 + k = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x-3)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 10 - k$.

Need $10 - k > 0$, so $k < 10$.

(b) The centre $(3, -1)$ is $1$ unit from the $x$-axis, so touching the axis means $r = 1$.

$10 - k = 1$, so $k = 9$.

Question 10

$A(-2, -2)$, $B(6, 4)$ and $C(5, 5)$.

(a) Show that $\angle ACB = 90°$.   (b) Hence find the equation of the circle through $A$, $B$ and $C$.   (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at $C$.   (d) The tangent meets the $x$-axis at $T$. Find the exact length $CT$.

▶ Show solution

(a) $m_{AC} = \dfrac{5 + 2}{5 + 2} = 1$,  $m_{BC} = \dfrac{5 - 4}{5 - 6} = -1$. Product $-1$, so $\angle ACB = 90°$.

(b) The angle in a semicircle is $90°$, so $AB$ is a diameter. Centre $M$ = midpoint of $AB$ = $(2, 1)$.

$r = \tfrac12 AB = \tfrac12\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \tfrac12(10) = 5$.

$(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 25$.  (Check $C$: $9 + 16 = 25$ ✓)

(c) $m_{MC} = \dfrac{5 - 1}{5 - 2} = \dfrac43$, so the tangent gradient is $-\tfrac34$.

$y - 5 = -\tfrac34(x - 5) \;\Rightarrow\; 3x + 4y - 35 = 0$

(d) $y = 0$: $3x = 35$, so $T\left(\tfrac{35}{3}, 0\right)$.

$CT^2 = \left(\tfrac{35}{3} - 5\right)^2 + (0 - 5)^2 = \tfrac{400}{9} + \tfrac{225}{9} = \tfrac{625}{9}$, so $CT = \tfrac{25}{3}$.

Check: triangle $MCT$ has a right angle at $C$, so $MT^2 = 5^2 + \left(\tfrac{25}{3}\right)^2 = \tfrac{850}{9}$, and directly $MT^2 = \left(\tfrac{29}{3}\right)^2 + 1^2 = \tfrac{850}{9}$ ✓

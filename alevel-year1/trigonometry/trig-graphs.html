1 Sine, Cosine and Tangent for Any Angle

At GCSE, $\sin\theta$ was "opposite over hypotenuse" — which only makes sense for angles between $0°$ and $90°$. At A Level we need $\sin 200°$ and $\cos(-45°)$, so we redefine the functions using a circle. This page is part of DfE content area E · Trigonometry; in Year 1 all angles are in degrees.

The unit-circle definition. Start on the positive $x$-axis and turn through $\theta$ (anticlockwise for positive $\theta$, clockwise for negative). The point reached on the circle of radius $1$ is $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ is the gradient of $OP$.
CAST. Reading anticlockwise from the fourth quadrant: Cos, All, Sin, Tan tells you which functions are positive in each quadrant. It is a memory aid; the graphs below show the same information and more.

Why $\tan$ has asymptotes. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ and $\cos\theta = 0$ at $90°$, $270°$, … so $\tan\theta$ is undefined there and the graph shoots off to $\pm\infty$.
3 Symmetry and Periodicity

Every useful fact about related angles can be read from the graphs.

PropertySineCosineTangent
Periodicity$\sin(\theta + 360°) = \sin\theta$$\cos(\theta + 360°) = \cos\theta$$\tan(\theta + 180°) = \tan\theta$
Negative angles$\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$$\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$$\tan(-\theta) = -\tan\theta$
Supplement$\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$$\cos(180° - \theta) = -\cos\theta$$\tan(180° - \theta) = -\tan\theta$
Half turn$\sin(180° + \theta) = -\sin\theta$$\cos(180° + \theta) = -\cos\theta$$\tan(180° + \theta) = \tan\theta$
Complement$\sin(90° - \theta) = \cos\theta$  and  $\cos(90° - \theta) = \sin\theta$—
Don't memorise the table — derive it. The cosine graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis, so $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$. The sine graph is symmetric about $\theta = 90°$, so $\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$. The cosine graph is the sine graph shifted $90°$ left: $\cos\theta = \sin(\theta + 90°)$.
Worked Example 1 — Using a known value

Given $\sin 20° \approx 0.342$, write down $\sin 160°$, $\sin 200°$, $\sin(-20°)$ and $\cos 70°$.

①$\sin 160° = \sin(180° - 20°) = \sin 20° \approx 0.342$
②$\sin 200° = \sin(180° + 20°) = -\sin 20° \approx -0.342$
③$\sin(-20°) = -\sin 20° \approx -0.342$
④$\cos 70° = \sin(90° - 70°) = \sin 20° \approx 0.342$
4 Exact Values

You must know these without a calculator. They come from two triangles: half of an equilateral triangle of side $2$ (angles $30°, 60°, 90°$, sides $1, \sqrt3, 2$) and an isosceles right-angled triangle with shorter sides $1$ (hypotenuse $\sqrt2$).

Worked Example 2 — Exact values beyond 90°

Find the exact values of (a) $\sin 240°$   (b) $\cos 135°$   (c) $\tan 330°$   (d) $\sin(-150°)$.

①(a) $240° = 180° + 60°$, third quadrant where sin is negative: $\sin 240° = -\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$
②(b) $135° = 180° - 45°$, second quadrant where cos is negative: $\cos 135° = -\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$
③(c) $330° = 360° - 30°$, fourth quadrant where tan is negative: $\tan 330° = -\tfrac{\sqrt3}{3}$
④(d) $\sin(-150°) = -\sin 150° = -\sin 30° = -\tfrac12$
5 Transformations of Trigonometric Graphs

The rules from Transformations of Graphs apply unchanged. With trig graphs, watch what each one does to the period and the amplitude.

EquationTransformation of $y = \sin x$Effect
$y = a\sin x$Stretch parallel to the $y$-axis, scale factor $a$Amplitude $a$; range $-a \leqslant y \leqslant a$
$y = \sin bx$Stretch parallel to the $x$-axis, scale factor $\tfrac1b$Period $\dfrac{360°}{b}$
$y = \sin(x + c)$Translation by $\begin{pmatrix} -c \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$Graph moves $c°$ left

Inside the bracket, everything works backwards. $y = \sin 2x$ squashes the graph (period $180°$), and $y = \sin(x - 30°)$ moves it $30°$ to the right.
Worked Example 3 — Sketching a transformed graph

Sketch $y = 2\cos(x - 30°) + 1$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$, giving the coordinates of the maximum and minimum points and of the $y$-intercept.

①Start with $y = \cos x$: max $(0°, 1)$, min $(180°, -1)$.
②$x - 30°$: translate $30°$ right. Max now at $x = 30°$, min at $x = 210°$.
③$\times 2$ then $+1$: the range becomes $-2 + 1 \leqslant y \leqslant 2 + 1$, i.e. $-1 \leqslant y \leqslant 3$.
④Maximum $(30°, 3)$, minimum $(210°, -1)$.
⑤$y$-intercept: $2\cos(-30°) + 1 = 2 \times \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} + 1 = 1 + \sqrt3 \approx 2.73$.
Worked Example 4 — Period and asymptotes of a tangent graph

State the period of $y = \tan 2x$ and the equations of its asymptotes for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$.

①$y = \tan x$ has period $180°$; the stretch factor $\tfrac12$ halves it to $90°$.
②Asymptotes where $2x = 90°, 270°, 450°, 630°$, i.e. $x = 45°, 135°, 225°, 315°$.
Worked Example 5 — Finding the equation from the graph

A curve $y = a\sin bx$ ($a, b > 0$) has its first maximum at $(30°, 5)$. Find $a$ and $b$.

①The maximum value is $5$, so the amplitude $a = 5$.
②$y = \sin x$ has its first maximum at $90°$, a quarter of the period. Here a quarter period is $30°$, so the period is $120°$.
③$\dfrac{360°}{b} = 120°$, so $b = 3$. The curve is $y = 5\sin 3x$.
Worked Example 6 — Counting solutions from a graph

How many solutions does $\sin 2x = 0.4$ have for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$?

①$y = \sin 2x$ has period $180°$, so it completes two full cycles on $0°$ to $360°$.
②Each cycle crosses the line $y = 0.4$ twice (since $-1 < 0.4 < 1$).
③So there are $4$ solutions. Finding them is the job of Trigonometric Equations.

Unit circle

$P = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$; $\tan\theta$ = gradient of $OP$.

CAST

Positive: All (Q1), Sin (Q2), Tan (Q3), Cos (Q4).

Periods

sin, cos: $360°$. tan: $180°$.

$\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$; $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$.

From the $1, \sqrt3, 2$ and $1, 1, \sqrt2$ triangles.

7 Practice Questions
Question 1

Write down the exact value of $\sin 300°$.

▶ Show solution

$300° = 360° - 60°$, fourth quadrant, where sin is negative.

$\sin 300° = -\sin 60° = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

Question 2

Find the exact values of $\cos 225°$ and $\tan 150°$.

▶ Show solution

$225° = 180° + 45°$ (third quadrant, cos negative): $\cos 225° = -\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.

$150° = 180° - 30°$ (second quadrant, tan negative): $\tan 150° = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}$.

Question 3

Given that $\cos 40° \approx 0.766$, write down the values of $\cos 320°$, $\cos 140°$ and $\sin 50°$.

▶ Show solution

$\cos 320° = \cos(360° - 40°) = \cos 40° \approx 0.766$

$\cos 140° = \cos(180° - 40°) = -\cos 40° \approx -0.766$

$\sin 50° = \cos(90° - 50°) = \cos 40° \approx 0.766$

Question 4

State the amplitude and the period of $y = 5\sin 3x$.

▶ Show solution

Amplitude $5$. Period $\dfrac{360°}{3} = 120°$.

Question 5

Describe fully the single transformation that maps $y = \cos x$ onto $y = \cos(x + 45°)$.

▶ Show solution

A translation by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} -45 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, i.e. $45°$ in the negative $x$-direction.

Question 6

Find the maximum and minimum values of $y = 3 - 2\sin x$, and the values of $x$ in $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$ where they occur.

▶ Show solution

$y$ is largest when $\sin x$ is smallest: $\sin x = -1$ at $x = 270°$, giving $y = 3 + 2 = 5$.

$y$ is smallest when $\sin x = 1$ at $x = 90°$, giving $y = 3 - 2 = 1$.

Maximum $5$ at $x = 270°$; minimum $1$ at $x = 90°$.

Question 7

For $y = \tan(x - 45°)$, $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$, find the asymptotes, the $x$-intercepts and the $y$-intercept.

▶ Show solution

The graph of $y = \tan x$ moves $45°$ right.

Asymptotes: $x - 45° = 90°, 270°$, so $x = 135°$ and $x = 315°$.

$x$-intercepts: $x - 45° = 0°, 180°$, so $x = 45°$ and $x = 225°$.

$y$-intercept: $\tan(-45°) = -1$, so $(0, -1)$.

Question 8

The graph of $y = a\cos bx$ ($a, b > 0$) has a maximum at $(0°, 4)$ and its next minimum at $(60°, -4)$. Find $a$ and $b$.

▶ Show solution

Amplitude $a = 4$.

Maximum to next minimum is half a period, so the period is $120°$.

$\dfrac{360°}{b} = 120°$, so $b = 3$.

Question 9

Without solving, state the number of solutions of (a) $\cos 3x = -0.2$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$ and (b) $2\sin x = 3$ for all $x$. Explain each answer.

▶ Show solution

(a) $y = \cos 3x$ has period $120°$, so it completes three cycles. Each cycle crosses $y = -0.2$ twice: $6$ solutions.

(b) $\sin x = 1.5$ is impossible because $-1 \leqslant \sin x \leqslant 1$: no solutions.

Question 10

The height $h$ metres of a seat on a big wheel, $t$ minutes after it passes the lowest point, is modelled by $h = 20 - 18\cos(12t)°$.

(a) Find the minimum and maximum heights.   (b) How long does one revolution take?   (c) Find the height after $5$ minutes.   (d) Find the first time at which the seat is $29$ m high.   (e) For how long in each revolution is the seat higher than $29$ m?

▶ Show solution

(a) $\cos(12t)°$ ranges from $-1$ to $1$, so $h$ ranges from $20 - 18 = 2$ m to $20 + 18 = 38$ m.

(b) Period $= \dfrac{360}{12} = 30$ minutes.

(c) $h = 20 - 18\cos 60° = 20 - 9 = 11$ m.

(d) $20 - 18\cos(12t)° = 29 \;\Rightarrow\; \cos(12t)° = -\tfrac12$.

The first angle with cosine $-\tfrac12$ is $120°$, so $12t = 120$ and $t = 10$ minutes.

(e) The next angle with cosine $-\tfrac12$ is $240°$, at $t = 20$. Between these, $\cos(12t)° < -\tfrac12$, so $h > 29$.

The seat is above $29$ m for $20 - 10 = 10$ minutes per revolution.

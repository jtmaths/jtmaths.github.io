1 The Big Idea

Trigonometry starts as the study of triangles and grows into the study of anything that repeats: tides, sound waves, a big wheel, alternating current. In Year 1 you make that jump — from "opposite over hypotenuse" to functions defined for every angle.

The key idea is the unit circle: the point at angle $\theta$ on a circle of radius $1$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. That single definition gives the graphs, their symmetries, both identities and the method for solving every equation.

$\sin^{-1}x$ is not $\dfrac{1}{\sin x}$. The $-1$ means "inverse function": $\sin^{-1} 0.5$ is the angle whose sine is $0.5$. But $\sin^2 x$ does mean $(\sin x)^2$. The notation is inconsistent; you just have to know it.
3 The Six Core Techniques
1 — The sine rule
$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$  — check $180° - \theta$ when finding an angle
2 — The cosine rule and area
$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$   ·   Area $= \tfrac12 ab\sin C$
3 — Exact values and related angles
Reference angle for the size, CAST or the graph for the sign
4 — Transforming trig graphs
$y = a\sin(bx + c) + d$: amplitude $a$, period $\dfrac{360°}{b}$
5 — The two identities
$\tan\theta \equiv \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$     $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$
6 — Solving equations
Principal value → second value from symmetry → add or subtract the period → check the interval
Worked Example — Several techniques in one question

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 5$ cm, $AC = 8$ cm and the area is $10\sqrt3$ cm$^2$. Find the two possible sizes of angle $A$, and the length $BC$ in the case where $A$ is obtuse.

①Area: $\tfrac12(5)(8)\sin A = 10\sqrt3 \;\Rightarrow\; \sin A = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.
②Solving the equation for $0° < A < 180°$: $A = 60°$ or $A = 180° - 60° = 120°$.
③Obtuse case, cosine rule: $BC^2 = 25 + 64 - 2(5)(8)\cos 120° = 89 - 80\left(-\tfrac12\right) = 129$.
④$BC = \sqrt{129} \approx 11.4$ cm.
This uses the area formula, an exact value, the symmetry $\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$ and the cosine rule. Questions that join two or three subtopics like this are the norm.

5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
Calculator in the wrong mode. $\sin 30 = -0.988$ means you are in radians. In Year 1, always degrees.
Stopping at the calculator's answer. $\sin x = 0.5$ has two solutions between $0°$ and $360°$, not one. The same applies to angles found with the sine rule.
Forgetting to transform the interval. For $\sin 2x = k$ with $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$, you need every solution with $0° \leqslant 2x \leqslant 720°$.
Cancelling a trig function. Dividing $\sin x\cos x = \sin x$ by $\sin x$ throws away all the solutions of $\sin x = 0$. Factorise instead.
Proving an identity by treating it as an equation. Work from one side to the other, using $\equiv$. Do not "do the same to both sides".
6 Quick Reference

Sine rule

$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B}$

Cosine rule

$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$

Area

$\tfrac12 ab\sin C$

Exact values

$\sin 30° = \tfrac12$, $\sin 45° = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, $\sin 60° = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

CAST

Which ratios are positive in each quadrant.

Periods

sin, cos $360°$; tan $180°$; $\sin bx$ has $\dfrac{360°}{b}$.

Identities

$\tan \equiv \dfrac{\sin}{\cos}$; $\sin^2 + \cos^2 \equiv 1$

Second solution

sin: $180° - \alpha$; cos: $360° - \alpha$; tan: $\alpha + 180°$.

Range

$-1 \leqslant \sin x \leqslant 1$, same for cos.

7 Practice Questions

Ten questions drawing on the whole Trigonometry topic. Work each one through before opening the solution.

Question 1

Write down the exact value of $\cos 210°$.

▶ Show solution

$210° = 180° + 30°$, third quadrant, where cos is negative.

$\cos 210° = -\cos 30° = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

Question 2

In triangle $ABC$, $A = 30°$, $B = 45°$ and $a = 10$ cm. Find the exact length of $b$.

▶ Show solution

$b = \dfrac{10\sin 45°}{\sin 30°} = \dfrac{10 \times \frac{\sqrt2}{2}}{\frac12} = 10\sqrt2$ cm.

Question 3

In this question you must show detailed reasoning. A triangle has sides $7$, $8$ and $13$. Find its largest angle.

▶ Show solution

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{49 + 64 - 169}{2(7)(8)} = \dfrac{-56}{112} = -\dfrac12$

$\theta = 120°$.

Question 4

Find the area of a triangle with sides $6$ cm and $10$ cm and included angle $150°$.

▶ Show solution

$\tfrac12(6)(10)\sin 150° = 30 \times \tfrac12 = 15$ cm$^2$.

Question 5

State the period of $y = \cos 4x$ and the coordinates of its first minimum point for $x > 0$.

▶ Show solution

Period $\dfrac{360°}{4} = 90°$.

$\cos u$ has its first minimum at $u = 180°$, so $4x = 180°$, giving $(45°, -1)$.

Question 6

Given that $\sin\theta = \tfrac13$ and $\theta$ is acute, find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

▶ Show solution

$\cos^2\theta = 1 - \tfrac19 = \tfrac89$; acute so $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt8}{3} = \dfrac{2\sqrt2}{3}$.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{1/3}{2\sqrt2/3} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt2} = \dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}$.

Question 7

Prove that $\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta \equiv 2\cos^2\theta - 1$.

▶ Show solution

LHS $\equiv \cos^2\theta - (1 - \cos^2\theta)$

$\equiv 2\cos^2\theta - 1 \equiv$ RHS  ■

Question 8

Solve $4\sin x = 3\cos x$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$.

▶ Show solution

$\tan x = \tfrac34$, so $x = 36.9°$ or $36.87° + 180° = 216.9°$.

Question 9

Solve $2\sin^2 x = 1 + \cos x$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$.

▶ Show solution

$2(1 - \cos^2 x) = 1 + \cos x \;\Rightarrow\; 2\cos^2 x + \cos x - 1 = 0$

$(2\cos x - 1)(\cos x + 1) = 0$

$\cos x = \tfrac12$: $x = 60°, 300°$.  $\cos x = -1$: $x = 180°$.

$x = 60°, 180°, 300°$

Question 10

A triangular field $ABC$ has $AB = 50$ m, $BC = 70$ m and $\angle ABC = 60°$.

(a) Show that $AC = 10\sqrt{39}$ m.   (b) Find the exact area of the field.   (c) Find $\angle BAC$.   (d) A straight path runs from $B$ to the nearest point of $AC$. Find its length.

▶ Show solution

(a) $AC^2 = 50^2 + 70^2 - 2(50)(70)\cos 60° = 2500 + 4900 - 3500 = 3900$

$AC = \sqrt{3900} = \sqrt{100 \times 39} = 10\sqrt{39}$ m  ■

(b) Area $= \tfrac12(50)(70)\sin 60° = 1750 \times \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = 875\sqrt3$ m$^2$ ($\approx 1520$ m$^2$).

(c) $\cos\angle BAC = \dfrac{50^2 + 3900 - 70^2}{2(50)(10\sqrt{39})} = \dfrac{1500}{1000\sqrt{39}} = 0.2402\ldots$

$\angle BAC = 76.1°$ (1 d.p.)

(d) The path is the perpendicular height $h$ from $B$ to $AC$. Area $= \tfrac12 \times AC \times h$:

$h = \dfrac{2 \times 875\sqrt3}{10\sqrt{39}} = \dfrac{175\sqrt3}{\sqrt{39}} = \dfrac{175}{\sqrt{13}} \approx 48.5$ m.

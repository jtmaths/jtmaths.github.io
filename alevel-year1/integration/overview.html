🌊 Integration Overview

A Level Maths Year 1 · Overview of the whole topic

Level 3 · Ages 16–17
1 The Big Idea

Integration is differentiation run backwards. If you know the gradient function of a curve, integration gets you back to the curve. And — remarkably — the same process measures the area under a curve. Those two facts, joined by the fundamental theorem of calculus, are the whole of this topic in Year 1.

Differentiation asks: "given $y$, what is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$?" Integration asks the reverse question: "given $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, what was $y$?" Because adding a constant does not change a gradient, the answer is only ever known up to a constant — the famous $+\,c$.

DfE content area H · Integration. At AS you integrate $x^n$ (for any rational $n \neq -1$) and sums of such terms, evaluate definite integrals, and use them to find areas. Year 2 extends this to $e^x$, $\dfrac1x$, trigonometric functions, substitution and integration by parts. Calculators can evaluate definite integrals — but when a question says "show detailed reasoning" or asks for an exact answer, you must write the integration and the substitution of limits in full.

3 The Five Core Techniques
1 — Integrating a power of $x$
$\displaystyle\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c, \qquad n \neq -1$
2 — Rewrite first
Expand brackets, split fractions and turn roots into indices before integrating
3 — Finding a curve from its gradient
Integrate $\mathrm{f}'(x)$, then substitute a known point to find $c$
4 — Evaluating a definite integral
$\displaystyle\int_a^b \mathrm{f}(x)\,dx = \Big[\mathrm{F}(x)\Big]_a^b = \mathrm{F}(b) - \mathrm{F}(a)$
5 — Areas
Area under a curve above the axis $= \displaystyle\int_a^b y\,dx$; below the axis the integral is negative
Area between curves $= \displaystyle\int_a^b (\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx$
Worked Example — Several techniques in one question

A curve has gradient $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 6x$ and passes through $(0, 4)$. Find its equation, and find the area of the region between the curve and the $x$-axis for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$.

①Integrate: $y = x^3 - 3x^2 + c$.
②At $(0, 4)$: $4 = 0 - 0 + c$, so $c = 4$ and $y = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$.
③Check the sign for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$: $y = (x+1)(x-2)^2 \geqslant 0$ there, so the curve stays above the axis.
④$\displaystyle\int_0^2 \left(x^3 - 3x^2 + 4\right)dx = \left[\tfrac14x^4 - x^3 + 4x\right]_0^2 = (4 - 8 + 8) - 0 = 4$
⑤The area is $4$ square units.
Step ③ is the one people skip. Before using an integral as an area, always ask whether the curve dips below the axis anywhere in the interval.

5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
Forgetting $+\,c$. Every indefinite integral needs it, and "find the equation of the curve" questions depend on it.
Integrating a product or quotient term by term. $\displaystyle\int (x+1)(x+2)\,dx$ is not $\left(\tfrac12x^2 + x\right)\left(\tfrac12x^2 + 2x\right)$. Expand first, then integrate.
Dividing by the old power. $\displaystyle\int x^4\,dx = \tfrac15x^5$, not $\tfrac14x^5$. Raise the power first, then divide by the new power.
Negative powers. $\displaystyle\int x^{-3}\,dx = \dfrac{x^{-2}}{-2} = -\dfrac{1}{2x^2}$. The power goes from $-3$ to $-2$ (up, not down), and the divisor is negative.
Treating a negative integral as an area. If part of the region is below the $x$-axis, integrate each part separately and add the sizes. A single integral across a root can even give zero.
Bracket errors with limits. $\mathrm{F}(b) - \mathrm{F}(a)$ subtracts the whole of $\mathrm{F}(a)$. Put it in brackets and take care with negative lower limits.
6 Quick Reference

Power rule

$\int x^n\,dx = \dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$, $n \neq -1$.

Constant multiple

$\int kx^n\,dx = \dfrac{k\,x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$.

Constant term

$\int k\,dx = kx + c$.

Sums

Integrate term by term.

Finding $c$

Substitute a known point after integrating.

Definite integral

$[\mathrm{F}(x)]_a^b = \mathrm{F}(b) - \mathrm{F}(a)$; no $c$.

Below the axis

Integral is negative; area is its modulus.

Between curves

$\int (\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx$ between intersections.

Check

Differentiate your answer: you should get the integrand back.

7 Practice Questions

Ten questions drawing on the whole Integration topic. Work each one through before opening the solution.

Question 1

Find $\displaystyle\int \left(8x^3 + 2\right)dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{8x^4}{4} + 2x + c = 2x^4 + 2x + c$

Question 2

Find $\displaystyle\int 5x^{3/2}\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

Add one to the power: $\tfrac32 + 1 = \tfrac52$.

$\dfrac{5x^{5/2}}{5/2} + c = 2x^{5/2} + c$

Question 3

Find $\displaystyle\int (x+1)(x+3)\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

Expand first: $(x+1)(x+3) = x^2 + 4x + 3$.

$\displaystyle\int \left(x^2 + 4x + 3\right)dx = \tfrac13x^3 + 2x^2 + 3x + c$

Question 4

A curve has $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 2$ and passes through $(1, 3)$. Find its equation.

▶ Show solution

$y = 2x^3 - 2x + c$

At $(1, 3)$: $3 = 2 - 2 + c$, so $c = 3$.

$y = 2x^3 - 2x + 3$

Question 5

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} \left(3x^2 - 2x + 1\right)dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\left[x^3 - x^2 + x\right]_{-1}^{2}$

$= (8 - 4 + 2) - (-1 - 1 - 1) = 6 - (-3) = 9$

Question 6

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^4 3\sqrt{x}\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

$3\sqrt{x} = 3x^{1/2}$, which integrates to $\dfrac{3x^{3/2}}{3/2} = 2x^{3/2}$.

$\left[2x^{3/2}\right]_1^4 = 2(8) - 2(1) = 14$

Question 7

Find the area of the region enclosed by the curve $y = 4 - x^2$ and the $x$-axis.

▶ Show solution

The curve meets the axis where $4 - x^2 = 0$, i.e. $x = \pm 2$, and lies above it in between.

$\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}\left(4 - x^2\right)dx = \left[4x - \tfrac13x^3\right]_{-2}^{2}$

$= \left(8 - \tfrac83\right) - \left(-8 + \tfrac83\right) = 16 - \tfrac{16}{3} = \dfrac{32}{3}$

Question 8

Find the area of the region between the curve $y = x^2 - 3x$ and the $x$-axis.

▶ Show solution

$x^2 - 3x = x(x - 3)$, which is zero at $x = 0$ and $x = 3$ and negative between them.

$\displaystyle\int_0^3 \left(x^2 - 3x\right)dx = \left[\tfrac13x^3 - \tfrac32x^2\right]_0^3 = 9 - \tfrac{27}{2} = -\tfrac92$

The negative sign shows the region is below the axis. Area $= \dfrac92$.

Question 9

Given that $\displaystyle\int_1^k 6x^2\,dx = 52$, find the value of $k$.

▶ Show solution

$\left[2x^3\right]_1^k = 2k^3 - 2 = 52$

$2k^3 = 54$, so $k^3 = 27$ and $k = 3$.

Question 10

A curve $y = \mathrm{f}(x)$ has $\mathrm{f}'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x$ and passes through the point $(0, 4)$.

(a) Find $\mathrm{f}(x)$.   (b) Show that $\mathrm{f}(x) = (x+1)(x-2)^2$.   (c) Sketch the curve, and find the exact area of the region bounded by the curve and the $x$-axis.

▶ Show solution

(a) $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + c$, and $\mathrm{f}(0) = 4$ gives $c = 4$. So $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$.

(b) $(x-2)^2 = x^2 - 4x + 4$, and $(x+1)\left(x^2 - 4x + 4\right) = x^3 - 4x^2 + 4x + x^2 - 4x + 4 = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$ ✓

(c) A positive cubic crossing the axis at $x = -1$ and touching it at $x = 2$ (the squared factor). Between these it lies above the axis, with a maximum at $(0, 4)$.

The bounded region is $-1 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$:

$\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2}\left(x^3 - 3x^2 + 4\right)dx = \left[\tfrac14x^4 - x^3 + 4x\right]_{-1}^{2}$

$= (4 - 8 + 8) - \left(\tfrac14 + 1 - 4\right) = 4 - \left(-\tfrac{11}{4}\right) = \dfrac{27}{4}$

The area is $\dfrac{27}{4}$ square units.

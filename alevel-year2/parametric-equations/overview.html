🌀 Parametric Equations Overview

A Level Maths Year 2 · Overview of the whole topic

Level 3 · Ages 17–18
1 The Big Idea

Up to now every curve has been described by a single equation linking $x$ and $y$. Parametric equations describe a curve differently: both $x$ and $y$ are given in terms of a third variable, the parameter.

Imagine a dot moving across the page. At each moment $t$ it has an $x$-coordinate and a $y$-coordinate. Writing $x = \mathrm{f}(t)$ and $y = \mathrm{g}(t)$ describes not just the path, but the journey along it.

2 The Language You Need
WordMeaningExample
ParameterThe third variable that $x$ and $y$ both depend on.$t$ in $x = t^2$, $y = 2t$
Parametric equationsThe pair of equations $x = \mathrm{f}(t)$, $y = \mathrm{g}(t)$.$x = 3\cos\theta$, $y = 3\sin\theta$
Cartesian equationAn equation linking $x$ and $y$ only.$x^2 + y^2 = 9$
EliminateRemove the parameter to get the Cartesian equation.$t = \tfrac{y}{2} \Rightarrow x = \tfrac{y^2}{4}$
Domain (of the curve)The set of $x$ values the curve covers.$x = \sin\theta$ gives $-1 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$
Range (of the curve)The set of $y$ values the curve covers.$y = t^2$ gives $y \geqslant 0$
The Cartesian equation can describe more than the curve. $x = \sin\theta$, $y = \cos 2\theta$ gives $y = 1 - 2x^2$, but only for $-1 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$. Always carry the restriction across.
3 The Four Core Techniques
1 — Plotting
Choose values of $t$, tabulate $x$ and $y$, plot and join in order of $t$
2 — Eliminating the parameter
Algebraic: make $t$ the subject of one equation and substitute
Trigonometric: isolate $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ (or $\tan\theta$, $\sec\theta$) and use an identity
3 — Domain and range
Find the possible $x$ and $y$ values from the allowed values of the parameter
4 — Intersections
Axes: solve $x(t) = 0$ or $y(t) = 0$  ·  a line: substitute $x(t)$, $y(t)$ into its equation
Worked Example — Several techniques in one question

A curve has equations $x = t^2 - 1$, $y = 2t$. (a) Find a Cartesian equation. (b) Find where it crosses the $y$-axis. (c) Find where it meets the line $y = x - 2$.

①(a) $t = \dfrac{y}{2}$, so $x = \dfrac{y^2}{4} - 1$, i.e. $y^2 = 4(x + 1)$.
②(b) $x = 0$: $t^2 = 1$, $t = \pm 1$, giving $(0, 2)$ and $(0, -2)$.
③(c) $2t = (t^2 - 1) - 2 \Rightarrow t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (t - 3)(t + 1) = 0$.
④$t = 3$: $(8, 6)$.   $t = -1$: $(0, -2)$. Check: $6 = 8 - 2$ ✓, $-2 = 0 - 2$ ✓.
For (c) you could use the Cartesian equation instead, but substituting the parametric forms gives a simpler equation in $t$ — and the points follow at once.

5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
Leaving the parameter in. A Cartesian equation must contain only $x$ and $y$.
Forgetting to square the coefficient. From $x = 3\cos\theta$, $\cos^2\theta = \dfrac{x^2}{9}$, not $\dfrac{x^2}{3}$.
Range from end-points only. For $y = t^2$ with $-1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$ the least value is $0$, at $t = 0$, not $1$.
Confusing paths crossing with objects meeting. Two moving objects collide only if they are at the same point for the same value of $t$.
Degrees and radians. In modelling with $\sin(\omega t)$, $\omega t$ is in radians unless you are told otherwise. Set your calculator accordingly.
6 Quick Reference

Parametric

$x = \mathrm{f}(t)$, $y = \mathrm{g}(t)$.

Eliminate

$t$ = …, then substitute.

Pythagorean

$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.

Also useful

$1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$; $\cos 2\theta = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$.

Circle

$x = a + r\cos\theta$, $y = b + r\sin\theta$.

Ellipse

$x = p\cos\theta$, $y = q\sin\theta$.

Restrictions

Domain and range come from the parameter.

Models

Interpret $t$; restrict it to sensible values.

7 Practice Questions

Ten questions covering the whole topic. Work each one through before opening the solution.

Question 1

Find a Cartesian equation for $x = 3t - 1$, $y = t + 2$.

▶ Show solution

$t = y - 2$, so $x = 3(y - 2) - 1 = 3y - 7$.

Question 2

For $x = t^2$, $y = t^3$, find the points where $t = 0, 1, 2$ and a Cartesian equation.

▶ Show solution

$(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(4, 8)$.

$x^3 = t^6 = y^2$, so $y^2 = x^3$.

Question 3

Describe the curve $x = 2\cos\theta$, $y = 2\sin\theta$, $0 \leqslant \theta < 2\pi$.

▶ Show solution

$x^2 + y^2 = 4$: a circle, centre the origin, radius $2$, traced once anticlockwise starting from $(2, 0)$.

Question 4

Find $y$ in terms of $x$ for $x = t - 1$, $y = \dfrac{1}{t}$, $t > 0$, and state the domain.

▶ Show solution

$t = x + 1$, so $y = \dfrac{1}{x + 1}$.

$t > 0$ means $x > -1$.

Question 5

Find a Cartesian equation for $x = 4\cos\theta$, $y = 3\sin\theta$.

▶ Show solution

$\cos\theta = \tfrac{x}{4}$, $\sin\theta = \tfrac{y}{3}$, so $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ (an ellipse).

Question 6

Find where the curve $x = t^2$, $y = 4t$ meets the line $y = x$.

▶ Show solution

$4t = t^2 \Rightarrow t(t - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 0$ or $t = 4$.

Points $(0, 0)$ and $(16, 16)$.

Question 7

A curve has $x = 3 + 2t$, $y = t^2$, $-1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$. Find the domain and range.

▶ Show solution

$x$ increases with $t$: domain $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 7$.

$y = t^2$: least $0$ at $t = 0$, greatest $4$ at $t = 2$. Range $0 \leqslant y \leqslant 4$.

Question 8

Find a Cartesian equation for $x = 2\sin\theta$, $y = \cos 2\theta$, stating the domain.

▶ Show solution

$\cos 2\theta = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$ and $\sin\theta = \tfrac{x}{2}$.

$y = 1 - 2\left(\tfrac{x}{2}\right)^2 = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2}$, for $-2 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$.

Question 9

Find a Cartesian equation for $x = \sec\theta$, $y = 2\tan\theta$.

▶ Show solution

$\sec^2\theta - \tan^2\theta = 1$, with $\sec\theta = x$ and $\tan\theta = \tfrac{y}{2}$.

$x^2 - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$.

Question 10

A ball is kicked from ground level. After $t$ seconds it is at $x = 8t$, $y = 10t - 5t^2$ (metres).

(a) Find when and where it lands.  (b) Find its greatest height.  (c) Find the Cartesian equation of its path, with its domain.  (d) Give one limitation of the model.

▶ Show solution

(a) $5t(2 - t) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 2$ s; then $x = 16$ m.

(b) $y = -5(t - 1)^2 + 5$: greatest height $5$ m at $t = 1$ s.

(c) $t = \tfrac{x}{8}$: $y = \dfrac{10x}{8} - \dfrac{5x^2}{64} = \dfrac{5x}{4} - \dfrac{5x^2}{64}$, for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 16$.

(Check: $x = 16$ gives $20 - 20 = 0$ ✓; $x = 8$ gives $10 - 5 = 5$ ✓.)

(d) It ignores air resistance (or treats the ball as a particle, or ignores spin).

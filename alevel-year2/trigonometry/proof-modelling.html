1 Proving Identities with the Full Toolkit
To prove $\text{LHS} \equiv \text{RHS}$, start with one side — usually the more complicated one — and transform it step by step until it becomes the other side. Do not work on both sides at once, and never start by assuming what you are trying to prove.
ToolIdentities
Definitions$\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$,  $\sec x = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$,  $\operatorname{cosec} x = \dfrac{1}{\sin x}$,  $\cot x = \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$
Pythagorean$\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x \equiv 1$,  $1 + \tan^2 x \equiv \sec^2 x$,  $1 + \cot^2 x \equiv \operatorname{cosec}^2 x$
Compound angle$\sin(A \pm B)$,  $\cos(A \pm B)$,  $\tan(A \pm B)$
Double angle$\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$,  $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$
  • If you cannot see a route, rewrite everything in terms of $\sin$ and $\cos$.
  • Combine fractions over a common denominator.
  • Look for $1 - \sin^2 x$, $1 - \cos^2 x$, $1 \pm \cos 2x$ — they simplify.
  • Factorise and cancel. Keep the target in view: if the answer has $\cot x$, you want $\cos x$ over $\sin x$.
Worked Example 1 — Combining fractions

Prove that $\dfrac{\cos x}{1 - \sin x} + \dfrac{\cos x}{1 + \sin x} \equiv 2\sec x$.

①LHS $= \dfrac{\cos x(1 + \sin x) + \cos x(1 - \sin x)}{(1 - \sin x)(1 + \sin x)}$
②$= \dfrac{2\cos x}{1 - \sin^2 x} = \dfrac{2\cos x}{\cos^2 x}$
③$= \dfrac{2}{\cos x} = 2\sec x = $ RHS $\blacksquare$
Worked Example 2 — Double angles and reciprocals

Prove that $\operatorname{cosec} 2x + \cot 2x \equiv \cot x$.

①LHS $= \dfrac{1}{\sin 2x} + \dfrac{\cos 2x}{\sin 2x} = \dfrac{1 + \cos 2x}{\sin 2x}$
②$= \dfrac{2\cos^2 x}{2\sin x\cos x}$  (choosing $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$ to cancel the $1$)
③$= \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x} = \cot x = $ RHS $\blacksquare$
Worked Example 3 — Spotting a compound angle

Prove that $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{\sin x} - \dfrac{\cos 3x}{\cos x} \equiv 2$.

①LHS $= \dfrac{\sin 3x\cos x - \cos 3x\sin x}{\sin x\cos x}$
②The numerator is $\sin(3x - x) = \sin 2x$, and the denominator is $\tfrac12\sin 2x$.
③LHS $= \dfrac{\sin 2x}{\frac12\sin 2x} = 2 = $ RHS $\blacksquare$
Expanding $\sin 3x$ and $\cos 3x$ as cubics also works, but takes four times as long. Look for a pattern before you expand.
2 Solving Equations in Radians

The method is the same as in Year 1; what changes is the toolkit for getting to a single function, and the interval is usually in radians.

Worked Example 4 — Many solutions

Solve $2\sin 2\theta = 3\tan\theta$ for $0 \leqslant \theta \leqslant 2\pi$.

①$4\sin\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{3\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. Multiply by $\cos\theta$ (non-zero wherever $\tan\theta$ is defined): $4\sin\theta\cos^2\theta = 3\sin\theta$.
②$\sin\theta(4\cos^2\theta - 3) = 0$.
③$\sin\theta = 0$: $\theta = 0, \pi, 2\pi$.
④$\cos\theta = \pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$: $\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{6}, \tfrac{5\pi}{6}, \tfrac{7\pi}{6}, \tfrac{11\pi}{6}$.
⑤Seven solutions: $0, \tfrac{\pi}{6}, \tfrac{5\pi}{6}, \pi, \tfrac{7\pi}{6}, \tfrac{11\pi}{6}, 2\pi$.
The $\pm$ in step ④ doubles the number of answers. Dropping the negative root is a very common way to lose marks.
Worked Example 5 — A quadratic via an identity

Solve $2\cos^2 x + 3\sin x = 3$ for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2\pi$.

①$2(1 - \sin^2 x) + 3\sin x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow 2\sin^2 x - 3\sin x + 1 = 0$.
②$(2\sin x - 1)(\sin x - 1) = 0$.
③$\sin x = \tfrac12$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{6}, \tfrac{5\pi}{6}$.  $\sin x = 1$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$.
3 Trigonometric Models

Anything that repeats regularly — tides, a Ferris wheel, hours of daylight, a vibrating spring — can be modelled by a sine or cosine function.

The general model
$y = A + B\sin(\omega t + c)$  or  $y = A + B\cos(\omega t + c)$
centre line $A$ · amplitude $|B|$ · maximum $A + |B|$, minimum $A - |B|$ · period $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$
Finding $\omega$. If one full cycle takes $T$ units of time, then $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$. A 12-hour tide gives $\omega = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$; a 365-day year gives $\omega = \tfrac{2\pi}{365}$.
Worked Example 6 — Tides

The depth of water in a harbour is $D = 5 + 3\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right)$ metres, $t$ hours after midnight. A ship needs at least $6.5$ m of water. For how long in each tidal cycle can it use the harbour?

①Period $= \dfrac{2\pi}{\pi/6} = 12$ hours. Maximum depth $8$ m (at $t = 0$), minimum $2$ m (at $t = 6$).
②$5 + 3\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{6}\right) \geqslant 6.5 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{6}\right) \geqslant \tfrac12$.
③For one cycle $0 \leqslant \tfrac{\pi t}{6} \leqslant 2\pi$: true when $\tfrac{\pi t}{6} \leqslant \tfrac{\pi}{3}$ or $\tfrac{\pi t}{6} \geqslant \tfrac{5\pi}{3}$.
④So $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$ or $10 \leqslant t \leqslant 12$: a total of $4$ hours in every $12$.
Worked Example 7 — A Ferris wheel

A Ferris wheel has radius $20$ m and its centre is $22$ m above the ground. It turns once every $10$ minutes. A passenger boards at the lowest point at $t = 0$. (a) Find a model for the height $h$ m after $t$ minutes. (b) Find the height after $2$ minutes. (c) For how long in each revolution is the passenger more than $30$ m above the ground?

①(a) Starts at the minimum, so use $-\cos$: $\omega = \tfrac{2\pi}{10} = \tfrac{\pi}{5}$, and $h = 22 - 20\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{5}\right)$.
②Check: $t = 0$ gives $h = 2$ m (bottom); $t = 5$ gives $h = 42$ m (top) ✓
③(b) $h = 22 - 20\cos(0.4\pi) = 22 - 6.18 = 15.8$ m (3 s.f.).
④(c) $22 - 20\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{5}\right) > 30 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{5}\right) < -0.4$.
⑤$\arccos(-0.4) = 1.9823$, so $1.9823 < \tfrac{\pi t}{5} < 2\pi - 1.9823 = 4.3009$.
⑥$3.155 < t < 6.845$: the passenger is above $30$ m for $3.69$ minutes per revolution.
Worked Example 8 — Hours of daylight

The number of hours of daylight in a UK city on day $t$ of the year is modelled by $H = 12 + 4.5\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi(t - 80)}{365}\right)$. (a) Find the greatest number of hours of daylight and the day it occurs. (b) For how many days is there more than $15$ hours of daylight? (c) Give one limitation of the model.

①(a) Greatest $H = 16.5$ hours, when $\dfrac{2\pi(t - 80)}{365} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, i.e. $t = 80 + 91.25 = 171.25$ — about day $171$ (20 June).
②(b) $4.5\sin(\ldots) > 3 \Rightarrow \sin(\ldots) > \tfrac23$. $\arcsin\tfrac23 = 0.7297$.
③$0.7297 < \dfrac{2\pi(t - 80)}{365} < \pi - 0.7297 = 2.4119$, so $42.39 < t - 80 < 140.11$.
④$122.4 < t < 220.1$: days $123$ to $220$, which is $98$ days.
⑤(c) Real daylight hours are not a perfect sine curve, and the model ignores leap years and the effect of the clocks changing on daily routines.
4 Quick Reference

Proof layout

Start from one side; end on the other. Finish with $\blacksquare$.

Stuck?

Convert to $\sin$ and $\cos$; combine fractions.

$1 \pm \cos 2x$

$1 + \cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x$;  $1 - \cos 2x = 2\sin^2 x$

Equations

Factorise; keep both $\pm$ roots.

Period

$\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ for $\sin(\omega t + c)$.

Max / min

$A \pm |B|$ for $A + B\sin(\ldots)$.

Start at the bottom

Use $A - B\cos(\omega t)$.

Duration

Solve the inequality for one full cycle.

5 Practice Questions
Question 1

Prove that $(\sin x + \cos x)^2 \equiv 1 + \sin 2x$.

▶ Show solution

LHS $= \sin^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x = 1 + \sin 2x = $ RHS $\blacksquare$

Question 2

Prove that $\dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{1 + \cos 2x} \equiv \tan^2 x$.

▶ Show solution

LHS $= \dfrac{2\sin^2 x}{2\cos^2 x} = \tan^2 x = $ RHS $\blacksquare$

Question 3

Prove that $\cot x - \tan x \equiv 2\cot 2x$.

▶ Show solution

LHS $= \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x} - \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \dfrac{\cos^2 x - \sin^2 x}{\sin x\cos x} = \dfrac{\cos 2x}{\frac12\sin 2x} = 2\cot 2x = $ RHS $\blacksquare$

Question 4

Prove that $\tan x + \cot x \equiv 2\operatorname{cosec} 2x$.

▶ Show solution

LHS $= \dfrac{\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x}{\sin x\cos x} = \dfrac{1}{\sin x\cos x} = \dfrac{2}{\sin 2x} = 2\operatorname{cosec} 2x$ $\blacksquare$

Question 5

Solve $\sin 2x = \cos x$ for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2\pi$.

▶ Show solution

$2\sin x\cos x - \cos x = 0 \Rightarrow \cos x(2\sin x - 1) = 0$.

$\cos x = 0$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$.  $\sin x = \tfrac12$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{6}, \tfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

Question 6

Solve $\tan 2x = 3\tan x$ for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant \pi$.

▶ Show solution

Let $t = \tan x$: $\dfrac{2t}{1 - t^2} = 3t \Rightarrow 2t = 3t - 3t^3 \Rightarrow t(3t^2 - 1) = 0$.

$t = 0$: $x = 0, \pi$.  $t = \pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{6}, \tfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

(None of these makes $1 - t^2 = 0$, so all are valid.)

Question 7

For $y = 3 + 2\sin\left(4t - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$, state the amplitude, period, maximum value, and the smallest positive $t$ at which the maximum occurs.

▶ Show solution

Amplitude $2$; period $\dfrac{2\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$; maximum $3 + 2 = 5$.

$4t - \tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac{\pi}{2} \Rightarrow 4t = \tfrac{5\pi}{6} \Rightarrow t = \tfrac{5\pi}{24}$.

Question 8

The displacement of a mass on a spring is $x = 4\cos 2t + 3\sin 2t$ cm, $t$ seconds after release. Find the amplitude of the motion and the first time at which $x = 0$.

▶ Show solution

$x = R\cos(2t - \alpha)$ with $R\cos\alpha = 4$, $R\sin\alpha = 3$: $R = 5$ cm, $\alpha = \arctan\tfrac34 = 0.6435$.

$x = 0$ first when $2t - 0.6435 = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$, so $t = \dfrac{1.5708 + 0.6435}{2} = 1.11$ s (3 s.f.).

Question 9

A Ferris wheel of radius $15$ m has its centre $17$ m above the ground and turns once every $8$ minutes. A passenger boards at the bottom at $t = 0$. Write down a model for the height $h$ m after $t$ minutes, and find the first time the passenger is $25$ m above the ground.

▶ Show solution

$\omega = \tfrac{2\pi}{8} = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$, and starting at the bottom: $h = 17 - 15\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{4}\right)$.

$17 - 15\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{4}\right) = 25 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{4}\right) = -\tfrac{8}{15}$.

$\tfrac{\pi t}{4} = \arccos\left(-\tfrac{8}{15}\right) = 2.1333$, so $t = \dfrac{4 \times 2.1333}{\pi} = 2.72$ minutes (3 s.f.).

Question 10

The depth of water at a harbour entrance is modelled by $D = 7 + 2.5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6.2}\right)$ metres, where $t$ is the time in hours after midnight.

(a) State the period of the model and explain why it is realistic for tides.   (b) Find the maximum and minimum depths and the first times at which they occur.   (c) A boat needs a depth of more than $8$ m. Find the time window during the first tidal cycle when it can enter, to the nearest minute.   (d) Suggest one way in which the model could be refined.

▶ Show solution

(a) Period $= \dfrac{2\pi}{\pi/6.2} = 12.4$ hours. Real tides have two highs and two lows a day, about $12$ hours $25$ minutes apart, so this matches well.

(b) Maximum $9.5$ m when $\tfrac{\pi t}{6.2} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$, i.e. $t = 3.1$ h (03:06). Minimum $4.5$ m when $\tfrac{\pi t}{6.2} = \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$, i.e. $t = 9.3$ h (09:18).

(c) $2.5\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{6.2}\right) > 1 \Rightarrow \sin\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{6.2}\right) > 0.4$. $\arcsin 0.4 = 0.4115$.

$0.4115 < \tfrac{\pi t}{6.2} < \pi - 0.4115 = 2.7301$, so $0.812 < t < 5.388$.

The boat can enter between 00:49 and 05:23 — a window of about $4$ hours $35$ minutes.

(d) The heights of high and low tide vary over a lunar month (spring and neap tides), so the amplitude $2.5$ could itself be made to vary with time; or the model could be fitted to real tide-table data.

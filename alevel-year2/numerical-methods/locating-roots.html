1 Equations You Cannot Solve Exactly

Most equations that come out of real problems — $x^3 - 3x - 1 = 0$, $\mathrm{e}^{-x} = x$, $x\sin x = 1$ — have no neat algebraic solution. We can still pin the answer down as accurately as we like. The first step is always to trap the root inside a small interval.

A root of the equation $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ is a value of $x$ where the graph of $y = \mathrm{f}(x)$ meets the $x$-axis. We usually call an unknown root $\alpha$ (alpha).
The change of sign principle
If $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous for $a \leqslant x \leqslant b$ and $\mathrm{f}(a)$ and $\mathrm{f}(b)$ have opposite signs,
then $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ has at least one root $\alpha$ with $a < \alpha < b$

The reason is visual: a continuous graph has no gaps or jumps, so to get from below the axis to above it the curve must cross the axis somewhere in between.

Always use radians when the function involves $\sin$, $\cos$ or $\tan$. Set your calculator to radian mode before you start; a sign check in degrees is worthless.
2 Showing That a Root Lies in an Interval

Exam questions say "Show that the equation … has a root between $a$ and $b$". A full answer has three parts, and the third is the one people forget.

  1. Rearrange the equation into the form $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ and define $\mathrm{f}(x)$.
  2. Calculate $\mathrm{f}(a)$ and $\mathrm{f}(b)$ and write down their values (a sign alone is not enough).
  3. Conclude: "There is a change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous for $a \leqslant x \leqslant b$, so there is a root between $a$ and $b$."
Worked Example 1 — A cubic

Show that $x^3 - 3x - 1 = 0$ has a root $\alpha$ with $1 < \alpha < 2$.

①Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 3x - 1$.
②$\mathrm{f}(1) = 1 - 3 - 1 = -3 < 0$
③$\mathrm{f}(2) = 8 - 6 - 1 = 1 > 0$
④There is a change of sign, and $\mathrm{f}$ is a polynomial so it is continuous. Therefore there is a root between $1$ and $2$.
Worked Example 2 — Rearranging first

Show that the equation $x \sin x = 1$ has a root between $x = 1$ and $x = 1.2$.

①Rearrange: $\mathrm{f}(x) = x\sin x - 1$, so roots of $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ are solutions of the equation.
②In radians: $\mathrm{f}(1) = \sin 1 - 1 = -0.1585\ldots < 0$
③$\mathrm{f}(1.2) = 1.2\sin 1.2 - 1 = 0.1184\ldots > 0$
④Change of sign, and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous, so there is a root with $1 < x < 1.2$.
You cannot test "$x\sin x = 1$" for a sign change directly — the equation has no sign. Always make one side zero first.
Worked Example 3 — Counting roots with a sketch

By sketching $y = 2^x$ and $y = 3x$, explain why $2^x = 3x$ has exactly two roots, and locate each between consecutive integers.

①$y = 3x$ is a straight line through the origin; $y = 2^x$ starts above it at $(0, 1)$ but eventually grows faster than any line. The exponential is below the line in the middle and above it at both ends, so the graphs cross twice.
②Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = 2^x - 3x$. Then $\mathrm{f}(0) = 1$, $\mathrm{f}(1) = -1$: a root with $0 < x < 1$.
③$\mathrm{f}(3) = 8 - 9 = -1$, $\mathrm{f}(4) = 16 - 12 = 4$: a root with $3 < x < 4$.
Sketching the two sides of an equation is the quickest way to know how many roots to look for, before you start hunting with signs.
3 Narrowing Down: Roots to a Given Accuracy

Once a root is trapped, you can shrink the interval by testing values in between — a decimal search. Each new sign check tells you which half of the interval the root is in.

Worked Example 4 — A decimal search

Find the root of $x^3 - 3x - 1 = 0$ between $1$ and $2$ correct to $2$ decimal places.

$x$$1.8$$1.9$$1.87$$1.88$$1.875$
$\mathrm{f}(x)$$-0.568$$0.159$$-0.0708$$0.00467$$-0.0332$
①Sign change between $1.8$ and $1.9$, so $1.8 < \alpha < 1.9$.
②Sign change between $1.87$ and $1.88$, so $1.87 < \alpha < 1.88$.
③Check the midpoint: $\mathrm{f}(1.875) < 0$, so $1.875 < \alpha < 1.88$.
④Every number in that interval rounds to $1.88$, so $\alpha = 1.88$ to $2$ d.p.
Proving a root is correct to a given accuracy
To show $\alpha = 1.796$ to $3$ d.p., test the bounds $1.7955$ and $1.7965$ and find a change of sign
Worked Example 5 — Using the bounds

Show that $1.796$ is a root of $x^3 - x - 4 = 0$ correct to $3$ decimal places.

①Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - x - 4$. The bounds for $1.796$ to $3$ d.p. are $1.7955$ and $1.7965$.
②$\mathrm{f}(1.7955) = -0.00713\ldots < 0$
③$\mathrm{f}(1.7965) = 0.00155\ldots > 0$
④Change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ continuous, so $1.7955 < \alpha < 1.7965$, and hence $\alpha = 1.796$ to $3$ d.p.
Testing $1.795$ and $1.797$ is not enough: a root at $1.7952$ would lie between them but round to $1.795$. Only the half-way bounds prove the rounding.
Worked Example 6 — A transcendental equation

Show that the equation $\mathrm{e}^{-x} = x$ has a root $\alpha = 0.567$ correct to $3$ decimal places.

①Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = \mathrm{e}^{-x} - x$.
②$\mathrm{f}(0.5665) = 0.00101\ldots > 0$ and $\mathrm{f}(0.5675) = -0.00056\ldots < 0$
③Change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous, so $0.5665 < \alpha < 0.5675$ and $\alpha = 0.567$ to $3$ d.p.
The sign may go from positive to negative — that is still a change of sign.
4 When the Change of Sign Method Fails

The principle has two halves: a sign change with continuity guarantees a root, and no sign change guarantees nothing. Both halves can trip you up.

SituationWhat goes wrongExample
Vertical asymptoteSign changes but there is no root — the function is not continuous.$\dfrac{1}{x-2}$ for $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 3$
Repeated rootThe curve touches the axis without crossing, so there is no sign change.$(x-1)^2(x+2)$ for $0.5 \leqslant x \leqslant 1.5$
Two (or an even number of) roots close togetherThe sign changes twice, so the end values have the same sign.$(x-0.3)(x-0.7)$ for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$
Interval too wideA sign change may hide three roots, not one.$x^3 - 3x - 1$ for $-2 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$
Worked Example 7 — A false root

$\mathrm{f}(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 2}$. Show that $\mathrm{f}(1)$ and $\mathrm{f}(3)$ have opposite signs, and explain why $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ has no root with $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 3$.

①$\mathrm{f}(1) = -1$ and $\mathrm{f}(3) = 1$: opposite signs.
②But $\dfrac{1}{x-2}$ is never zero — a fraction is only zero when its numerator is.
③The sign change happens because $\mathrm{f}$ is undefined at $x = 2$: there is a vertical asymptote. $\mathrm{f}$ is not continuous for $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 3$, so the principle does not apply.
Worked Example 8 — A root with no sign change

$\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2$. Find $\mathrm{f}(0.5)$ and $\mathrm{f}(1.5)$, and comment.

①$\mathrm{f}(0.5) = 0.125 - 1.5 + 2 = 0.625$ and $\mathrm{f}(1.5) = 3.375 - 4.5 + 2 = 0.875$. Both positive.
②Yet $\mathrm{f}(1) = 1 - 3 + 2 = 0$: there is a root in the interval.
③$\mathrm{f}(x) = (x-1)^2(x+2)$. The squared factor means the curve touches the axis at $x = 1$ and turns back, so the sign never changes.
No sign change does not mean no root. Repeated roots are exactly where change of sign methods are blind — a sketch or factorising will reveal them.
5 Quick Reference

The principle

$\mathrm{f}$ continuous for $a \leqslant x \leqslant b$ and $\mathrm{f}(a)\mathrm{f}(b) < 0$ $\Rightarrow$ a root with $a < x < b$.

Set up

Rearrange to $\mathrm{f}(x) = 0$ before testing signs.

Show the values

Write $\mathrm{f}(a)$ and $\mathrm{f}(b)$ as numbers, then conclude.

Conclusion

"Change of sign, $\mathrm{f}$ continuous, so a root lies in the interval."

Accuracy

For $\alpha = 1.796$ (3 d.p.), test $1.7955$ and $1.7965$.

Radians

Always, for trigonometric functions.

Asymptote

Sign change but no root — discontinuity.

Repeated root

Root but no sign change — the curve touches.

Sketch first

Tells you how many roots to look for.

6 Practice Questions
Question 1

Show that $x^3 - 5x + 1 = 0$ has a root between $x = 2$ and $x = 3$.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 5x + 1$.

$\mathrm{f}(2) = 8 - 10 + 1 = -1 < 0$ and $\mathrm{f}(3) = 27 - 15 + 1 = 13 > 0$.

There is a change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous, so there is a root between $2$ and $3$.

Question 2

Show that the equation $\mathrm{e}^x = 4 - x$ has a root with $1 < x < 1.1$.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = \mathrm{e}^x + x - 4$.

$\mathrm{f}(1) = \mathrm{e} - 3 = -0.2817\ldots < 0$

$\mathrm{f}(1.1) = \mathrm{e}^{1.1} - 2.9 = 0.1042\ldots > 0$

Change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous, so there is a root with $1 < x < 1.1$.

Question 3

Show that the equation $\ln x = 3 - x$ has a root between $2$ and $3$.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = \ln x + x - 3$, which is continuous for $x > 0$.

$\mathrm{f}(2) = \ln 2 - 1 = -0.3068\ldots < 0$ and $\mathrm{f}(3) = \ln 3 = 1.0986\ldots > 0$.

Change of sign, so there is a root with $2 < x < 3$.

Question 4

Show that the equation $x\sin x = 1$ has a root $\alpha = 1.11$ correct to $2$ decimal places.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = x\sin x - 1$ (radians). Test the bounds $1.105$ and $1.115$.

$\mathrm{f}(1.105) = -0.0127\ldots < 0$ and $\mathrm{f}(1.115) = 0.00117\ldots > 0$.

Change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ is continuous, so $1.105 < \alpha < 1.115$, giving $\alpha = 1.11$ to $2$ d.p.

Question 5

Show that $x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0$ has a root $\alpha = 2.095$ correct to $3$ decimal places.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 2x - 5$. Test $2.0945$ and $2.0955$.

$\mathrm{f}(2.0945) = -0.000575\ldots < 0$

$\mathrm{f}(2.0955) = 0.0106\ldots > 0$

Change of sign, $\mathrm{f}$ continuous, so $2.0945 < \alpha < 2.0955$ and $\alpha = 2.095$ to $3$ d.p.

Note: $\mathrm{f}(2.0945)$ is very small — the root ($2.09455\ldots$) is only just above the lower bound. That is why you must work to enough significant figures.

Question 6

A student finds that $\tan 1 > 0$ and $\tan 2 < 0$ and concludes that $\tan x = 0$ has a root between $1$ and $2$. Explain the error.

▶ Show solution

$\tan 1 = 1.557\ldots$ and $\tan 2 = -2.185\ldots$, so the signs are indeed different.

But $\tan x$ has a vertical asymptote at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1.5707\ldots$, which lies between $1$ and $2$. The function is not continuous for $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$, so the change of sign principle does not apply.

In fact the roots of $\tan x = 0$ are $x = 0, \pm\pi, \ldots$ — none lies between $1$ and $2$.

Question 7

$\mathrm{f}(x) = 4x^2 - 4x + 1$. Show that $\mathrm{f}(0)$ and $\mathrm{f}(1)$ have the same sign. Does this mean there is no root with $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$?

▶ Show solution

$\mathrm{f}(0) = 1$ and $\mathrm{f}(1) = 4 - 4 + 1 = 1$, both positive.

No: $\mathrm{f}(x) = (2x - 1)^2$, which has a repeated root at $x = 0.5$. The graph touches the axis there without crossing, so there is no sign change even though there is a root.

Question 8

$\mathrm{f}(x) = x^3 - 3x - 1$. Given $\mathrm{f}(-2) = -3$ and $\mathrm{f}(2) = 1$, a student says "there is one root between $-2$ and $2$". By evaluating $\mathrm{f}(-1)$ and $\mathrm{f}(0)$, show that there are in fact three roots, and give an interval of width $1$ for each.

▶ Show solution

$\mathrm{f}(-1) = -1 + 3 - 1 = 1$ and $\mathrm{f}(0) = -1$.

$x$$-2$$-1$$0$$1$$2$
$\mathrm{f}(x)$$-3$$1$$-1$$-3$$1$

Sign changes for $-2 < x < -1$, for $-1 < x < 0$ and for $1 < x < 2$: three roots. A cubic has at most three, so these are all of them.

The sign change between $-2$ and $2$ only guarantees at least one root.

Question 9

The equation $2^x = 3x$ has two roots. Show that the larger root lies between $3.3$ and $3.4$.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{f}(x) = 2^x - 3x$.

$\mathrm{f}(3.3) = 2^{3.3} - 9.9 = -0.0508\ldots < 0$

$\mathrm{f}(3.4) = 2^{3.4} - 10.2 = 0.356\ldots > 0$

Change of sign and $\mathrm{f}$ continuous, so a root lies in $3.3 < x < 3.4$. (The other root satisfies $0 < x < 1$, as in Worked Example 3.)

Question 10

The amount of a drug in a patient's bloodstream, in mg, $t$ hours after an injection is modelled by $D = 20t\,\mathrm{e}^{-0.5t}$. The drug is effective while $D \geqslant 10$.

(a) Show that $D = 10$ when $20t\,\mathrm{e}^{-0.5t} - 10 = 0$ has a root between $t = 0.7$ and $t = 0.8$.
(b) Show that there is a second root between $t = 4$ and $t = 5$.
(c) Show that the first root is $0.71$ correct to $2$ decimal places.
(d) Given that the second root is $4.31$ to $2$ d.p., estimate for how long the drug is effective.

▶ Show solution

Let $\mathrm{g}(t) = 20t\,\mathrm{e}^{-0.5t} - 10$, which is continuous.

(a) $\mathrm{g}(0.7) = -0.134\ldots < 0$ and $\mathrm{g}(0.8) = 0.725\ldots > 0$. Change of sign, so a root lies in $0.7 < t < 0.8$.

(b) $\mathrm{g}(4) = 0.827\ldots > 0$ and $\mathrm{g}(5) = -1.79\ldots < 0$. Change of sign, so a root lies in $4 < t < 5$.

(c) $\mathrm{g}(0.705) = -0.0887\ldots < 0$ and $\mathrm{g}(0.715) = 0.00174\ldots > 0$. Change of sign, so the root lies in $0.705 < t < 0.715$ and equals $0.71$ to $2$ d.p.

(d) $D \geqslant 10$ between the two roots, so the drug is effective for about $4.31 - 0.71 = 3.6$ hours.

Check: at $t = 2$ (between the roots) $D = 40\mathrm{e}^{-1} = 14.7 > 10$ ✓

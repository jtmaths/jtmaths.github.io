1 The Standard Results

In Year 1 you could only integrate powers of $x$ (see Indefinite Integration). Every derivative you learned in Exponentials, Logs and Trig now gives an integral, simply by reading it backwards.

Function $\mathrm{f}(x)$$\displaystyle\int \mathrm{f}(x)\,dx$Because…
$x^n \;(n \neq -1)$$\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$Year 1 result
$e^x$$e^x + c$$\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x$
$\dfrac{1}{x}$$\ln|x| + c$$\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = \dfrac{1}{x}$
$\cos x$$\sin x + c$$\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$
$\sin x$$-\cos x + c$$\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$
$\sec^2 x$$\tan x + c$$\dfrac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x$
Why the modulus in $\ln|x|$? $\dfrac{1}{x}$ exists for negative $x$ too, but $\ln x$ does not. For $x < 0$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(-x) = \dfrac{-1}{-x} = \dfrac{1}{x}$, so $\ln|x|$ covers both sides of the asymptote at once.
Also worth knowing: reading the other trig derivatives backwards gives $\int \operatorname{cosec}^2 x\,dx = -\cot x + c$, $\int \sec x \tan x\,dx = \sec x + c$ and $\int \operatorname{cosec} x \cot x\,dx = -\operatorname{cosec} x + c$. All trig integration is in radians.
Worked Example 1 — Sums and multiples

Find $\displaystyle\int \left(3e^{2x} + \frac{4}{x} - 5\cos x\right)dx$.

①Integrate term by term, keeping the constants as multipliers.
②$\int 3e^{2x}\,dx = \tfrac{3}{2}e^{2x}$ (the $2x$ is dealt with in Section 2).
③$\int \dfrac{4}{x}\,dx = 4\ln|x|$ and $\int -5\cos x\,dx = -5\sin x$.
④$= \tfrac{3}{2}e^{2x} + 4\ln|x| - 5\sin x + c$
2 The Reverse Chain Rule: $\mathrm{f}(ax+b)$

Differentiating $\mathrm{F}(ax+b)$ by the chain rule gives $a\,\mathrm{F}'(ax+b)$ — an extra factor of $a$. So when integrating you must divide by $a$.

Linear inside function
If $\displaystyle\int \mathrm{f}(x)\,dx = \mathrm{F}(x) + c$  then  $\displaystyle\int \mathrm{f}(ax+b)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\,\mathrm{F}(ax+b) + c$
IntegrandIntegral
$(ax+b)^n,\; n \neq -1$$\dfrac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)} + c$
$e^{ax+b}$$\dfrac{1}{a}e^{ax+b} + c$
$\dfrac{1}{ax+b}$$\dfrac{1}{a}\ln|ax+b| + c$
$\cos(ax+b)$$\dfrac{1}{a}\sin(ax+b) + c$
$\sin(ax+b)$$-\dfrac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b) + c$
$\sec^2(ax+b)$$\dfrac{1}{a}\tan(ax+b) + c$
Only for a linear inside. $\int e^{x^2}\,dx$ is not $\dfrac{e^{x^2}}{2x}$. Dividing by the derivative of the inside only works when that derivative is a constant.
Worked Example 2 — A power of a linear bracket

Find $\displaystyle\int (2x+1)^5\,dx$.

①Raise the power: $(2x+1)^6$, divide by the new power $6$.
②Divide by $a = 2$ for the inside.
③$= \dfrac{(2x+1)^6}{12} + c$
Check by differentiating: $\dfrac{6(2x+1)^5 \times 2}{12} = (2x+1)^5$ ✓. Do this every time — it takes seconds.
Worked Example 3 — A reciprocal of a linear expression

Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{3x-2}\,dx$, and evaluate $\displaystyle\int_2^5 \frac{3}{x-1}\,dx$ exactly.

①$\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{3x-2}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln|3x-2| + c$
②$\displaystyle\int_2^5 \frac{3}{x-1}\,dx = \Big[3\ln|x-1|\Big]_2^5 = 3\ln 4 - 3\ln 1$
③$= 3\ln 4 = 6\ln 2$
A definite integral of $\dfrac{1}{x-1}$ must not cross $x = 1$. The integrand is undefined there, so limits such as $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ are not allowed.
Worked Example 4 — A definite trig integral

Find the exact value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/6} \sin 3x\,dx$.

①$\displaystyle\int \sin 3x\,dx = -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos 3x$
②$\Big[-\tfrac{1}{3}\cos 3x\Big]_0^{\pi/6} = -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos\tfrac{\pi}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3}\cos 0$
③$= 0 + \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{1}{3}$
3 Recognising $\dfrac{\mathrm{f}'(x)}{\mathrm{f}(x)}$
Derivative over function
$\displaystyle\int \frac{\mathrm{f}'(x)}{\mathrm{f}(x)}\,dx = \ln|\mathrm{f}(x)| + c$
Why it works: by the chain rule $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln|\mathrm{f}(x)| = \dfrac{1}{\mathrm{f}(x)} \times \mathrm{f}'(x)$. So whenever the numerator is the derivative of the denominator — or a constant multiple of it — the answer is a logarithm.
Worked Example 5 — Adjusting the constant

Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^3 + 1}\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int \tan x\,dx$.

①$\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3+1) = 3x^2$. The numerator is $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of this.
②$\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^3+1}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\int\frac{3x^2}{x^3+1}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln|x^3+1| + c$
③$\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$, and $\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$.
④$\displaystyle\int \tan x\,dx = -\int\frac{-\sin x}{\cos x}\,dx = -\ln|\cos x| + c = \ln|\sec x| + c$
Worked Example 6 — A definite log integral

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{x}{x^2+1}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln 2$.

①$\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2+1) = 2x$, so the integrand is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{2x}{x^2+1}$.
②$\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+1)\Big]_0^1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln 2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln 1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln 2$ ✓
$x^2+1 > 0$ always, so the modulus signs can be dropped.
4 Recognising $\mathrm{f}'(x)\,[\mathrm{f}(x)]^n$
Derivative times a power of the function
$\displaystyle\int \mathrm{f}'(x)\,[\mathrm{f}(x)]^n\,dx = \frac{[\mathrm{f}(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1} + c, \quad n \neq -1$

This is the chain rule reversed again: treat the bracket as a single "lump", raise its power by one and divide by the new power. It only works if the derivative of the inside is sitting outside (up to a constant).

Worked Example 7 — Two recognitions

Find (a) $\displaystyle\int x(x^2+3)^4\,dx$   (b) $\displaystyle\int \sin x\cos^3 x\,dx$.

①(a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2+3) = 2x$, and we have $x$ — half of it.
②Guess $(x^2+3)^5$: its derivative is $5(x^2+3)^4 \times 2x = 10x(x^2+3)^4$. Too big by $10$.
③$\displaystyle\int x(x^2+3)^4\,dx = \frac{(x^2+3)^5}{10} + c$
④(b) $\cos^3 x = (\cos x)^3$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$.
⑤Guess $\cos^4 x$: derivative $-4\sin x\cos^3 x$. Adjust by $-\tfrac{1}{4}$.
⑥$\displaystyle\int \sin x\cos^3 x\,dx = -\tfrac{1}{4}\cos^4 x + c$
Guess, differentiate, adjust. This "inspection" method is quicker than a formal substitution and is fully acceptable — but only a constant can be adjusted, never a variable.
5 Using Trigonometric Identities

There is no standard result for $\sin^2 x$, $\cos^2 x$ or $\tan^2 x$. Rewrite them first using identities from Compound and Double Angles.

Identities for integration
$\sin^2 x \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2x)$  ·  $\cos^2 x \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x)$
$\tan^2 x \equiv \sec^2 x - 1$  ·  $\sin x\cos x \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x$
Worked Example 8 — Squares of trig functions

Find $\displaystyle\int \sin^2 x\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int \cos^2 3x\,dx$, and evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}\tan^2 x\,dx$ exactly.

①$\displaystyle\int \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2x)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}x - \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 2x + c$
②$\cos^2 3x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 6x)$ — the angle doubles to $6x$.
③$\displaystyle\int \cos^2 3x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}x + \tfrac{1}{12}\sin 6x + c$
④$\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}(\sec^2 x - 1)\,dx = \Big[\tan x - x\Big]_0^{\pi/4}$
⑤$= \left(1 - \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right) - 0 = 1 - \tfrac{\pi}{4}$
$\int \sin^2 x\,dx$ is not $\tfrac{1}{3}\sin^3 x$. Differentiating $\tfrac{1}{3}\sin^3 x$ gives $\sin^2 x\cos x$ — there is an extra $\cos x$. The "power of a function" rule needs the derivative of the inside to be present.
6 Quick Reference

Exponential

$\int e^{kx}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{k}e^{kx} + c$

Reciprocal

$\int \tfrac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + c$

Trig

$\int\cos kx = \tfrac{1}{k}\sin kx$; $\int \sin kx = -\tfrac{1}{k}\cos kx$

sec²

$\int \sec^2 kx\,dx = \tfrac{1}{k}\tan kx + c$

Linear inside

$\int \mathrm{f}(ax+b)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{a}\mathrm{F}(ax+b) + c$

f′/f

$\int \tfrac{\mathrm{f}'}{\mathrm{f}}\,dx = \ln|\mathrm{f}(x)| + c$

f′fⁿ

$\int \mathrm{f}'\mathrm{f}^n\,dx = \tfrac{\mathrm{f}^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$

sin² and cos²

Use $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 \mp \cos 2x)$ first.

tan²

Use $\sec^2 x - 1$, giving $\tan x - x$.

Always

Radians; $+\,c$; check by differentiating.

7 Practice Questions
Question 1

Find $\displaystyle\int \left(4e^x - \frac{3}{x} + 2\sin x\right)dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\int 4e^x\,dx = 4e^x$,  $\int -\tfrac{3}{x}\,dx = -3\ln|x|$,  $\int 2\sin x\,dx = -2\cos x$.

$= 4e^x - 3\ln|x| - 2\cos x + c$

Question 2

Find $\displaystyle\int \left(e^{3x-1} + \cos 4x\right)dx$.

▶ Show solution

Both have a linear inside, so divide by its coefficient.

$= \tfrac{1}{3}e^{3x-1} + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 4x + c$

Question 3

Find the exact value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/8}\sec^2 2x\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}\tan 2x\Big]_0^{\pi/8} = \tfrac{1}{2}\tan\tfrac{\pi}{4} - \tfrac{1}{2}\tan 0 = \tfrac{1}{2}(1) - 0 = \tfrac{1}{2}$

Question 4

Show that $\displaystyle\int_1^3 \frac{2}{2x+1}\,dx = \ln\tfrac{7}{3}$.

▶ Show solution

The numerator $2$ is exactly the derivative of $2x+1$, so $\displaystyle\int \frac{2}{2x+1}\,dx = \ln|2x+1|$.

$\Big[\ln(2x+1)\Big]_1^3 = \ln 7 - \ln 3 = \ln\tfrac{7}{3}$ ✓

Question 5

Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^3+1}\,dx$ for $x > -1$, then use the same idea to find $\displaystyle\int \frac{6x+3}{x^2+x+5}\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3+1) = 3x^2$, so $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^3+1}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln(x^3+1) + c$.

$\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2+x+5) = 2x+1$, and $6x + 3 = 3(2x+1)$.

$\displaystyle\int \frac{6x+3}{x^2+x+5}\,dx = 3\ln(x^2+x+5) + c$ (no modulus needed: $x^2+x+5 > 0$ since its discriminant is $1 - 20 < 0$).

Question 6

By writing $\cot x = \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$, show that $\displaystyle\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/2}\cot x\,dx = \ln 2$.

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$, so $\displaystyle\int \cot x\,dx = \ln|\sin x| + c$.

$\Big[\ln\sin x\Big]_{\pi/6}^{\pi/2} = \ln 1 - \ln\tfrac{1}{2} = 0 + \ln 2 = \ln 2$ ✓

Question 7

Find $\displaystyle\int x^2(x^3-1)^5\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

The inside $x^3 - 1$ has derivative $3x^2$; we have $x^2$.

Guess $(x^3-1)^6$: derivative $6(x^3-1)^5 \times 3x^2 = 18x^2(x^3-1)^5$.

So $\displaystyle\int x^2(x^3-1)^5\,dx = \frac{(x^3-1)^6}{18} + c$.

Question 8

In this question you must show detailed reasoning. Find the exact value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2 x\,dx$.

▶ Show solution

$\cos^2 x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x)$

$\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\cos 2x)\,dx = \Big[\tfrac{1}{2}x + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 2x\Big]_0^{\pi/2}$

$= \left(\tfrac{\pi}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin\pi\right) - 0 = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$

Question 9

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}(\sin x + \cos x)^2\,dx = \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{1}{2}$.

▶ Show solution

$(\sin x + \cos x)^2 = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x = 1 + \sin 2x$

$\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}(1 + \sin 2x)\,dx = \Big[x - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2x\Big]_0^{\pi/4}$

$= \left(\tfrac{\pi}{4} - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) - \left(0 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{4} + \tfrac{1}{2}$ ✓

Question 10

Water flows into a tank at a rate of $R = 6e^{-0.2t} + 2$ litres per minute, where $t$ is the time in minutes, for $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 10$. The tank is empty at $t = 0$.

(a) Explain why the volume of water in the tank after $T$ minutes is $\displaystyle\int_0^T R\,dt$.   (b) Find the exact volume after $5$ minutes and give it to 3 significant figures.   (c) Show that the volume after $10$ minutes is $50 - 30e^{-2}$ litres.   (d) Describe what happens to the rate of flow if the model were continued for a long time.

▶ Show solution

(a) $R = \dfrac{dV}{dt}$, so the volume is found by integrating the rate. The area under the rate–time graph from $0$ to $T$ is the total amount that has flowed in.

(b) $\displaystyle\int (6e^{-0.2t} + 2)\,dt = \frac{6}{-0.2}e^{-0.2t} + 2t = -30e^{-0.2t} + 2t$

$\Big[-30e^{-0.2t} + 2t\Big]_0^5 = (-30e^{-1} + 10) - (-30) = 40 - 30e^{-1}$

$= 40 - \dfrac{30}{e} \approx 29.0$ litres.

(c) $\Big[-30e^{-0.2t} + 2t\Big]_0^{10} = (-30e^{-2} + 20) + 30 = 50 - 30e^{-2}$ ✓  ($\approx 45.9$ litres)

(d) As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-0.2t} \to 0$, so $R \to 2$: the flow settles to a steady $2$ litres per minute.

# Integration

Extending integration to exponentials, logarithms and trig functions, and building the toolkit of techniques — substitution, parts and partial fractions — that turns hard integrals into standard ones.
The topic finishes with areas between curves and with solving differential equations to model real situations.

* [An Overview](overview.html) — choosing the right method, the notation, and the common mistakes

## The content statements

* [Standard Integrals](standard-integrals.html) — eˣ, 1/x and trig functions, the reverse chain rule, f′(x)/f(x), and integrating with trig identities
* [Integration by Substitution](substitution.html) — choosing a substitution, replacing *dx*, and changing the limits of a definite integral
* [Integration by Parts](by-parts.html) — the reverse of the product rule, choosing *u*, repeated parts and integrating ln *x*
* [Integrating with Partial Fractions](partial-fractions.html) — splitting a fraction with linear or repeated linear factors and integrating each piece
* [Areas and Integration as a Limit](areas.html) — the integral as the limit of a sum, areas between curves, and areas under parametric curves
* [Differential Equations](differential-equations.html) — separating the variables, general and particular solutions, and modelling growth, decay and cooling

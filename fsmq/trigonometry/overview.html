1 The Big Idea

At GCSE, $\sin$, $\cos$ and $\tan$ were ratios of sides in a right-angled triangle. At Level 3 they become functions defined for every angle, with graphs, identities and equations of their own.

That shift is the whole point of this section. A right-angled triangle cannot have an angle of $150°$, yet $\sin 150° = 0.5$ is perfectly meaningful. Understanding why unlocks the ambiguous case of the sine rule and the solving of trigonometric equations.
Measured in degrees only
The specification is explicit: angles in this qualification are in degrees.
Radians are not required. Check your calculator is in DEG mode.
A calculator left in radians is the single most costly setting error in this paper. Test it: $\sin 30$ should give $0.5$. If it gives $-0.988$, you are in radians.

3 The Key Formulae
Right-angled triangles (revision)
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$  ·  $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$  ·  $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$
Pythagoras: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$
The sine rule (PT2)
$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$
The cosine rule (PT2)
$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$,  rearranged as  $\cos A = \dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}$
Area of a triangle
$\text{Area} = \tfrac12 ab \sin C$
The two identities (PT3, PT4)
$\tan\theta \equiv \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$  ·  $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$
Which rule to use
Right-angled? → SOHCAHTOA and Pythagoras
Two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides? → cosine rule
A matching side-and-angle pair? → sine rule
Worked Example — Choosing the right tool

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 7$ cm, $BC = 9$ cm and angle $B = 62°$. Find $AC$ and then angle $A$.

①Two sides and the angle between them $\to$ cosine rule.
②$AC^2 = 7^2 + 9^2 - 2(7)(9)\cos 62°$
③$= 49 + 81 - 126 \times 0.46947 = 130 - 59.153 = 70.847$
④$AC = 8.417 = 8.42$ cm (3 s.f.)
⑤Now there is a matching pair ($AC$ with $B$), so use the sine rule for angle $A$:
⑥$\dfrac{\sin A}{9} = \dfrac{\sin 62°}{8.417}$, so $\sin A = \dfrac{9 \times 0.88295}{8.417} = 0.94410$
⑦$A = 70.7°$ (1 d.p.)
Keep unrounded values on your calculator. Using $8.42$ instead of $8.417$ in step ⑥ shifts the final angle. Store $AC$ in a memory and recall it.

5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
The calculator in radians. Check with $\sin 30° = 0.5$ before you start.
Missing the second solution. $\sin^{-1}$ gives only one answer. If the question asks for solutions in $0°$ to $360°$, there are usually two.
Missing the ambiguous case. When the sine rule finds an angle and the triangle is not fixed by other information, check whether $180° - \theta$ also works.
Rounding too early. Carry full accuracy through multi-step triangle problems; round only the final answer.
Assuming a triangle is right-angled because it looks it. Only use SOHCAHTOA when a right angle is stated or proved.
6 Quick Reference

Degrees

Always. Check DEG mode.

Sine rule

Needs a matching side–angle pair.

Cosine rule

Two sides and the included angle, or all three sides.

Area

$\tfrac12 ab\sin C$, with $C$ between $a$ and $b$.

Identity 1

$\tan\theta \equiv \tfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$.

Identity 2

$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$.

Periods

$\sin$, $\cos$: $360°$. $\tan$: $180°$.

Second solution

Sine: $180° - \theta$. Cosine: $360° - \theta$.

Ambiguous case

Check $180° - \theta$ still fits the triangle.

3-D problems

Redraw the relevant triangle flat, on its own.

7 Practice Questions
Question 1

A right-angled triangle has legs $5$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.

▶ Show solution

$c^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$, so $c = 13$ cm.

Question 2

Find $x$ in a right-angled triangle where the hypotenuse is $10$ cm and the angle between it and $x$ is $35°$.

▶ Show solution

$x$ is adjacent to the $35°$ angle, so $\cos 35° = \dfrac{x}{10}$.

$x = 10\cos 35° = 8.19$ cm (3 s.f.)

Question 3

In triangle $ABC$, $a = 8$, $A = 50°$ and $B = 70°$. Find $b$.

▶ Show solution

Sine rule: $\dfrac{b}{\sin 70°} = \dfrac{8}{\sin 50°}$

$b = \dfrac{8 \sin 70°}{\sin 50°} = \dfrac{8 \times 0.93969}{0.76604} = 9.81$ (3 s.f.)

Question 4

A triangle has sides $6$, $7$ and $9$. Find its largest angle.

▶ Show solution

The largest angle is opposite the longest side, $9$.

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{36 + 49 - 81}{2(6)(7)} = \dfrac{4}{84} = 0.047619$

$\theta = 87.3°$ (1 d.p.)

Question 5

Find the area of a triangle with sides $8$ cm and $11$ cm enclosing an angle of $40°$.

▶ Show solution

Area $= \tfrac12(8)(11)\sin 40° = 44 \times 0.64279$

$= 28.3$ cm² (3 s.f.)

Question 6

Given $\sin\theta = 0.6$ and $\theta$ is acute, find $\cos\theta$ exactly.

▶ Show solution

$\cos^2\theta = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$

$\cos\theta = 0.8$ (positive, since $\theta$ is acute).

Question 7

Solve $\sin x = 0.5$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$.

▶ Show solution

$\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30°$

The second solution is $180° - 30° = 150°$.

$x = 30°$ or $150°$.

Question 8

State the period and amplitude of $y = 5\sin(2x)$.

▶ Show solution

Amplitude $5$; period $\dfrac{360°}{2} = 180°$.

Question 9

The specification's own example: in triangle $ABC$, $AB = 10$ m, $AC = 8$ m and angle $B = 40°$. Find the two possible values of angle $C$.

▶ Show solution

We know side $AC = 8$ is opposite angle $B = 40°$, and side $AB = 10$ is opposite angle $C$.

Sine rule: $\dfrac{\sin C}{10} = \dfrac{\sin 40°}{8}$

$\sin C = \dfrac{10 \times 0.64279}{8} = 0.80349$

$C = 53.5°$  or  $C = 180° - 53.5° = 126.5°$ (1 d.p.)

Both are valid: adding $40°$ to either still leaves a positive third angle ($86.5°$ or $13.5°$), so two different triangles fit the data. This is the ambiguous case.

Question 10

A vertical mast $BT$ stands on horizontal ground. From a point $A$ on the ground, $50$ m from the base $B$, the angle of elevation of the top $T$ is $32°$.

(a) Find the height of the mast.   (b) A second point $C$ is $80$ m from $B$, with angle $ABC = 90°$. Find the distance $AC$.   (c) Find the angle of elevation of $T$ from $C$.   (d) Find the angle that $AT$ makes with the ground at $A$ — and explain why this is not the same as the angle $TAC$.

▶ Show solution

(a) In the right-angled triangle $ABT$: $\tan 32° = \dfrac{BT}{50}$

$BT = 50\tan 32° = 50 \times 0.62487 = 31.24$ m, so the mast is $31.2$ m tall (3 s.f.).

(b) Triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $B$, with $AB = 50$ and $BC = 80$:

$AC = \sqrt{50^2 + 80^2} = \sqrt{2500 + 6400} = \sqrt{8900} = 94.34$ m (2 d.p.)

(c) In the right-angled triangle $CBT$, with $CB = 80$ and $BT = 31.24$:

$\tan(\text{elevation}) = \dfrac{31.24}{80} = 0.39053$

Elevation $= 21.3°$ (1 d.p.)

(d) The angle $AT$ makes with the ground, measured in the vertical plane through $A$ and $B$, is the angle $TAB = 32°$ given in the question.

The angle $TAC$ is a different angle: it is measured in the plane containing $A$, $T$ and $C$, and $AC$ is a line along the ground in a different direction from $AB$.

To see the difference, note $AT = \dfrac{50}{\cos 32°} = 58.96$ m, while $CT = \sqrt{80^2 + 31.24^2} = 85.88$ m. In triangle $ATC$ we then have all three sides, so use the cosine rule:

$\cos(TAC) = \dfrac{AT^2 + AC^2 - CT^2}{2 \times AT \times AC} = \dfrac{3476 + 8900 - 7376}{2 \times 58.96 \times 94.34} = 0.4495$

$\angle TAC = 63.3°$ — very different from $32°$.

The lesson: in a 3-D problem the "angle of elevation" is always measured in the vertical plane containing the observer and the base of the object. An angle to a point in another direction is a genuinely different angle, and you must identify the correct triangle before calculating.

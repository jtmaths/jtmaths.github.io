1 Beyond the Right-Angled Triangle
PT1: use the definitions of $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ for any angle, and their graphs. This is the step that takes trigonometry past GCSE.

In a right-angled triangle an angle must be less than $90°$. But $\sin 200°$ and $\cos 340°$ are perfectly well defined. The trick is to stop thinking about triangles and think instead about a point going round a circle.

The unit circle definition
Take a point $P$ on a circle of radius $1$ centred at the origin,
with $\theta$ measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis. Then
$\cos\theta$ is the $x$-coordinate of $P$  ·  $\sin\theta$ is the $y$-coordinate of $P$
Which ratios are positive where
$0°$–$90°$: All positive  ·  $90°$–$180°$: Sine only
$180°$–$270°$: Tangent only  ·  $270°$–$360°$: Cosine only
Remember it as "All Students Take Care" going anticlockwise from the first quadrant: All, Sine, Tangent, Cosine.

The symmetries worth memorising
$\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$  ·  $\cos(360° - \theta) = \cos\theta$
$\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$  ·  $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$  ·  $\tan(180° + \theta) = \tan\theta$
Why $\cos$ is just $\sin$ shifted. The cosine graph is the sine graph translated $90°$ to the left: $\cos x = \sin(x + 90°)$. If you can draw one, you can draw the other.
3 Exact Values

When a question asks for an exact answer, a decimal from the calculator will not do. These values come from the two special triangles.

$\theta$$0°$$30°$$45°$$60°$$90°$
$\sin\theta$$0$$\dfrac12$$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$$\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$$1$
$\cos\theta$$1$$\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$$\dfrac12$$0$
$\tan\theta$$0$$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$$1$$\sqrt3$undefined
The two triangles. Half an equilateral triangle of side $2$ gives sides $1$, $\sqrt3$, $2$ with angles $30°$, $60°$, $90°$. A right-angled isosceles triangle with legs $1$ gives $1$, $1$, $\sqrt2$ with angles $45°$, $45°$, $90°$. Sketch them rather than memorising the table.
Worked Example 1 — An exact value of an obtuse angle

Find the exact value of $\sin 150°$ and of $\cos 150°$.

①$150°$ is in the second quadrant, where only sine is positive.
②The related acute angle is $180° - 150° = 30°$.
③$\sin 150° = +\sin 30° = \dfrac{1}{2}$
④$\cos 150° = -\cos 30° = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$
The universal method: find the related acute angle, look up its value, then attach the sign from CAST.
Worked Example 2 — A reflex angle

Find the exact value of $\tan 225°$.

①$225°$ is in the third quadrant, where tangent is positive.
②Related acute angle: $225° - 180° = 45°$.
③$\tan 225° = +\tan 45° = 1$
Worked Example 3 — Finding an angle from a negative ratio

Find all values of $\theta$ in $0° \leqslant \theta \leqslant 360°$ for which $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

①Ignore the sign for a moment: $\cos^{-1}\left(\tfrac12\right) = 60°$, the related acute angle.
②Cosine is negative in the second and third quadrants.
③Second quadrant: $180° - 60° = 120°$
④Third quadrant: $180° + 60° = 240°$
⑤$\theta = 120°$ or $240°$
Your calculator gives only one answer. $\cos^{-1}(-0.5)$ returns $120°$, and you must find $240°$ yourself from the symmetry.
4 Quick Reference

Definition

$(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ is a point on the unit circle.

CAST

All, Sine, Tangent, Cosine — anticlockwise.

Related angle

Find the acute angle, then attach the sign.

$\sin$ symmetry

$\sin(180°-\theta) = \sin\theta$.

$\cos$ symmetry

$\cos(360°-\theta) = \cos\theta$.

Periods

$\sin$, $\cos$: $360°$. $\tan$: $180°$.

Ranges

$\sin$, $\cos$: $[-1,1]$. $\tan$: everything.

$\tan$ asymptotes

Where $\cos\theta = 0$: $90°$, $270°$, …

Exact values

From the $30$–$60$–$90$ and $45$–$45$–$90$ triangles.

Impossible

$\sin\theta = 1.4$ has no solution.

5 Practice Questions
Question 1

In which quadrants is $\sin\theta$ negative?

▶ Show solution

The third and fourth — that is, $180° < \theta < 360°$.

From CAST, sine is positive only in the first and second quadrants.

Question 2

Write down the exact value of $\sin 60°$ and $\cos 45°$.

▶ Show solution

$\sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

$\cos 45° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$  (equivalently $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$)

Question 3

Find the exact value of $\cos 120°$.

▶ Show solution

Second quadrant, so cosine is negative. Related acute angle $180° - 120° = 60°$.

$\cos 120° = -\cos 60° = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Question 4

Find the exact value of $\sin 240°$.

▶ Show solution

Third quadrant, so sine is negative. Related acute angle $240° - 180° = 60°$.

$\sin 240° = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

Question 5

State the period and range of $y = \tan x$.

▶ Show solution

Period $180°$.

Range: all real values — $\tan x$ is unbounded, with asymptotes at $90°$, $270°$ and so on.

Question 6

Given $\sin 35° = 0.574$, write down the value of $\sin 145°$.

▶ Show solution

$145° = 180° - 35°$, and $\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$.

$\sin 145° = 0.574$

Question 7

Find all $\theta$ in $0° \leqslant \theta \leqslant 360°$ with $\sin\theta = -0.5$.

▶ Show solution

Related acute angle: $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30°$.

Sine is negative in the third and fourth quadrants:

$180° + 30° = 210°$  and  $360° - 30° = 330°$

$\theta = 210°$ or $330°$.

Question 8

Explain why $\cos\theta = 1.2$ has no solutions.

▶ Show solution

$\cos\theta$ is the $x$-coordinate of a point on the unit circle, so it can never exceed $1$ in magnitude.

The range of $\cos\theta$ is $-1 \leqslant \cos\theta \leqslant 1$, and $1.2$ lies outside it.

If a calculation ever produces this, you have made an arithmetic error earlier.

Question 9

Sketch $y = \sin x$ for $0° \leqslant x \leqslant 360°$ and use it to explain why $\sin x = 0.8$ has exactly two solutions in that interval.

▶ Show solution

The curve starts at $(0,0)$, rises to a maximum of $1$ at $90°$, falls back through $(180°, 0)$, down to $-1$ at $270°$, and returns to $(360°, 0)$.

The horizontal line $y = 0.8$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so it cuts the rising part of the first hump once and the falling part once — two crossings.

In the second half of the interval the curve is negative, so it never reaches $0.8$ again.

(Solving: $x = 53.1°$ and $x = 180° - 53.1° = 126.9°$.)

Question 10

$\theta$ is an angle with $\sin\theta = \tfrac{3}{5}$.

(a) If $\theta$ is acute, find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.   (b) If instead $\theta$ is obtuse, find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.   (c) Find both possible values of $\theta$, to 1 d.p.   (d) Explain why the question must tell you whether $\theta$ is acute or obtuse.

▶ Show solution

(a) Use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$:

$\cos^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{9}{25} = \dfrac{16}{25}$, so $\cos\theta = \pm\dfrac45$.

$\theta$ acute means the first quadrant, where cosine is positive: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{4}{5}$.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \dfrac{3/5}{4/5} = \dfrac{3}{4}$

(This is the familiar $3$–$4$–$5$ triangle.)

(b) $\theta$ obtuse means the second quadrant, where cosine is negative:

$\cos\theta = -\dfrac{4}{5}$  and  $\tan\theta = \dfrac{3/5}{-4/5} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$

Sine is unchanged at $\tfrac35$, consistent with CAST: sine is positive in both quadrants.

(c) $\sin^{-1}(0.6) = 36.9°$ (1 d.p.)

The obtuse solution is $180° - 36.9° = 143.1°$.

So $\theta = 36.9°$ or $143.1°$.

(d) Knowing $\sin\theta$ alone does not determine $\theta$: two angles in $0°$ to $180°$ share the same sine, because of the symmetry $\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$.

Those two angles have opposite-signed cosines and tangents, so without being told which one is meant, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ are genuinely ambiguous.

This is exactly the source of the ambiguous case of the sine rule.

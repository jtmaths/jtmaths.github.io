1 What "In Proportion" Means

Two situations are in proportion when their ratios are equal. That single idea is what lets you scale recipes, compare prices and solve for missing values.

A proportion statement
$a : b = c : d$   which is the same as   $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}$
Example. If $3$ apples cost £$1.20$, then $6$ apples cost £$2.40$.
The ratio apples : cost is $3 : 1.20$ in the first case and $6 : 2.40$ in the second.
Both simplify to $1 : 0.40$ — the ratios are equal, so the situations are in proportion.
2 Solving With Cross-Multiplication

When one of the four numbers is missing, the fastest reliable method is cross-multiplication.

Cross-multiplication
If $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}$  then  $a \times d = b \times c$
  • Write the proportion as two equal fractions, keeping like with like (e.g. cost on top of both sides, quantity underneath both).
  • Multiply diagonally across the equals sign, both ways.
  • Set the two products equal and solve the resulting equation.
  • Sense-check the size of your answer.
Keep like with like. $\dfrac{\text{cost}}{\text{quantity}} = \dfrac{\text{cost}}{\text{quantity}}$ is correct. $\dfrac{\text{cost}}{\text{quantity}} = \dfrac{\text{quantity}}{\text{cost}}$ is not.
Worked Example 1 — Finding a missing value

Solve $\;4 : 7 = 20 : x$.

①Write as fractions: $\dfrac{4}{7} = \dfrac{20}{x}$
②Cross-multiply: $4 \times x = 7 \times 20$
③$4x = 140$
④$x = 35$

Check: $4 : 7 = 20 : 35$? Divide the second by $5$: $4 : 7$ ✓

Worked Example 2 — A real context

A machine fills $150$ bottles in $8$ minutes. How long does it take to fill $525$ bottles?

①$\dfrac{\text{bottles}}{\text{minutes}}$ stays constant: $\dfrac{150}{8} = \dfrac{525}{t}$
②Cross-multiply: $150t = 8 \times 525 = 4200$
③$t = 4200 \div 150 = 28$

Answer: $28$ minutes

Sense check: $525$ is $3.5$ times $150$, and $8 \times 3.5 = 28$ ✓

3 The Unitary Method ("Find One")

An alternative that many students find clearer: scale down to one, then scale up.

Worked Example 3 — Unitary method

$7$ identical books weigh $1.96$ kg. What do $12$ such books weigh?

①Find one: $1.96 \div 7 = 0.28$ kg per book
②Scale up: $0.28 \times 12 = 3.36$ kg

Answer: $3.36$ kg

Which method? Use the unitary method when the "find one" step divides neatly. Use cross-multiplication when it does not — you avoid awkward recurring decimals in the middle of the working.
4 Ratio Tables and Double Number Lines

A ratio table lays the information out so the multipliers are obvious. Whatever you do to one row, do to the other.

Worked Example 4 — Using a ratio table

$5$ litres of paint cover $60\text{ m}^2$. How much paint is needed for $150\text{ m}^2$?

Paint (litres)$5$$1$$?$
Area (m²)$60$$12$$150$
①Divide both by $5$: $1$ litre covers $12\text{ m}^2$.
②$150 \div 12 = 12.5$

Answer: $12.5$ litres

In practice you would buy $13$ litres!

5 Best Buy Problems

"Which is better value?" questions are proportion questions. Make the comparison fair by reducing both options to the same base.

Two valid approaches:
• Cost per unit — divide price by quantity. The smaller answer is better value.
• Quantity per pound — divide quantity by price. The larger answer is better value.
Either is fine, but say clearly which one you are using.
Worked Example 5 — Best buy

Which is better value: a $750$ g box of cereal for £$2.40$, or a $1.2$ kg box for £$3.66$?

①Use cost per $100$ g to keep the numbers friendly.
②Small: $240\text{p} \div 7.5 = 32$p per $100$ g
③Large: $1.2$ kg $= 1200$ g, so $366\text{p} \div 12 = 30.5$p per $100$ g
④$30.5 < 32$

The $1.2$ kg box is better value — by $1.5$p per $100$ g.

Worked Example 6 — Special offers

Shop A sells juice at £$1.80$ per carton with "buy 3 get 1 free". Shop B sells the same juice at £$1.50$ per carton with "buy 2 get the third half price". Which shop is cheaper?

①Shop A: $4$ cartons cost $3 \times 1.80 = £5.40$, so $£5.40 \div 4 = £1.35$ each.
②Shop B: $3$ cartons cost $1.50 + 1.50 + 0.75 = £3.75$, so $£3.75 \div 3 = £1.25$ each.
③$£1.25 < £1.35$

Shop B is cheaper.

6 Deciding Whether Two Quantities Are in Proportion

To test a table of values, work out $\dfrac{y}{x}$ for every pair. If the answer is the same each time, the quantities are in direct proportion.

Worked Example 7 — Testing a table

Are $x$ and $y$ in direct proportion?

$x$$2$$5$$8$
$y$$7$$17.5$$28$
①$7 \div 2 = 3.5$
②$17.5 \div 5 = 3.5$
③$28 \div 8 = 3.5$

Yes — the ratio is constant, and $y = 3.5x$.

Worked Example 8 — A table that fails the test

A taxi charges £$3$ plus £$2$ per mile. Are cost and distance in proportion?

Miles$1$$2$$5$
Cost (£)$5$$7$$13$
①$5 \div 1 = 5$,  $7 \div 2 = 3.5$,  $13 \div 5 = 2.6$
②The ratios are not equal.

No, they are not in proportion — because of the fixed £$3$ charge. Doubling the distance does not double the cost.

The relationship is $C = 2m + 3$: a linear relationship, but not a proportional one (the graph does not pass through the origin).

7 Quick Reference

Definition

In proportion means equal ratios: $a : b = c : d$.

Cross-multiplying

$\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d} \Rightarrow ad = bc$. Keep like with like.

Unitary method

Divide to find one, then multiply up to the amount wanted.

Ratio tables

Do the same operation to both rows; work via $1$ if it helps.

Best buys

Compare cost per unit (smaller wins) or amount per £ (bigger wins).

Testing proportion

$\dfrac{y}{x}$ constant $\Rightarrow$ direct proportion. Any fixed charge breaks it.

8 Practice Questions
Question 1

Solve $\;3 : 8 = x : 40$.

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{x}{40}$

Cross-multiply: $8x = 3 \times 40 = 120$

$x = 15$

Question 2

Six identical chairs cost £$414$. How much do $11$ chairs cost?

▶ Show solution

One chair $= 414 \div 6 = £69$

Eleven chairs $= 69 \times 11 = £759$

Question 3

A recipe for $9$ muffins uses $360$ g of flour. How many muffins can be made with $600$ g of flour?

▶ Show solution

$\dfrac{9}{360} = \dfrac{m}{600}$

Cross-multiply: $360m = 9 \times 600 = 5400$

$m = 15$ muffins

Question 4

$4$ workers lay $180$ bricks in an hour. Working at the same rate, how many bricks would $7$ workers lay in an hour?

▶ Show solution

One worker lays $180 \div 4 = 45$ bricks per hour.

Seven workers lay $45 \times 7 = 315$ bricks per hour.

Question 5

Which is better value: $12$ eggs for £$3.24$, or $18$ eggs for £$4.68$?

▶ Show solution

Cost per egg (pack of 12): $324 \div 12 = 27$p

Cost per egg (pack of 18): $468 \div 18 = 26$p

The pack of $18$ is better value (1p cheaper per egg).

Question 6

Exchange rate: £$1$ = $$1.27$. Convert (a) £$350$ into dollars, (b) $$508$ into pounds.

▶ Show solution

(a) $350 \times 1.27 = 444.50$, so $$444.50$

(b) $508 \div 1.27 = 400$, so £$400$

Question 7

A $2.5$ litre tin of paint covers $30\text{ m}^2$. A decorator must paint a wall $12$ m long and $2.8$ m high, giving it two coats. How many tins must she buy?

▶ Show solution

Area of wall $= 12 \times 2.8 = 33.6\text{ m}^2$

Two coats: $33.6 \times 2 = 67.2\text{ m}^2$

Tins needed $= 67.2 \div 30 = 2.24$

Round up: she must buy $3$ tins.

Question 8

Decide whether $x$ and $y$ are in direct proportion. Justify your answer.

$x$$3$$4$$9$
$y$$10.5$$14$$31.5$
▶ Show solution

$10.5 \div 3 = 3.5$;  $14 \div 4 = 3.5$;  $31.5 \div 9 = 3.5$

Yes — the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$ is constant, so $y = 3.5x$.

Question 9

Shop A sells $6$ tins of soup for £$4.20$. Shop B sells the same tins at $80$p each with an offer of "buy 3 get 1 free". Which shop gives the cheaper tin?

▶ Show solution

Shop A: $420 \div 6 = 70$p per tin.

Shop B: you pay for $3$ tins and take home $4$.

Cost of $4$ tins $= 3 \times 80 = 240$p.

Cost per tin $= 240 \div 4 = 60$p.

Shop B is cheaper at $60$p per tin, saving $10$p on every tin.

Question 10

On a school trip the ratio of teachers to students must be at least $1 : 12$ (that is, no more than $12$ students per teacher).

(a) What is the smallest number of teachers needed for $150$ students?

(b) The trip costs £$18$ per student and £$0$ per teacher, plus a fixed coach hire of £$260$. Find the total cost.

▶ Show solution

(a) $150 \div 12 = 12.5$

You cannot have half a teacher, and $12$ teachers could only supervise $144$ students, so round up: $13$ teachers.

(b) Student cost $= 150 \times 18 = £2700$

Total $= 2700 + 260 = £2960$

Note: because of the fixed £$260$, the total cost is not in direct proportion to the number of students.

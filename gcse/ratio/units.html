1 Why Converting Units Matters

You cannot compare, add or simplify two quantities until they are measured in the same unit. Converting units is therefore the very first step in a huge number of GCSE questions.

A unit conversion is just a multiplication.
Every conversion means multiplying or dividing by a conversion factor.
Going to a smaller unit → the number gets bigger → multiply.
Going to a bigger unit → the number gets smaller → divide.
Worked Example 1 — Which way round?

Convert $3.6$ km into metres.

①A metre is smaller than a kilometre, so the number must get bigger.
②$1$ km $= 1000$ m, so multiply: $3.6 \times 1000 = 3600$
③Answer: $3600$ m

Sense check: 3600 is bigger than 3.6 ✓

2 Metric Units of Length, Mass and Capacity

The metric system is built on powers of $10$, so all conversions are $\times 10$, $\times 100$ or $\times 1000$.

MeasureConversions you must know
Length$1$ cm $= 10$ mm  ·  $1$ m $= 100$ cm $= 1000$ mm  ·  $1$ km $= 1000$ m
Mass$1$ g $= 1000$ mg  ·  $1$ kg $= 1000$ g  ·  $1$ tonne $= 1000$ kg
Capacity$1$ litre $= 1000$ ml  ·  $1$ litre $= 100$ cl  ·  $1$ cl $= 10$ ml
Link$1$ ml $= 1\text{ cm}^3$  ·  $1$ litre $= 1000\text{ cm}^3$  ·  $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000$ litres

Blue = going to a smaller unit (multiply) · Green = going to a bigger unit (divide)

Worked Example 2 — A two-step conversion

Convert $47\,500$ mm into kilometres.

①mm → m: divide by $1000$.   $47\,500 \div 1000 = 47.5$ m
②m → km: divide by $1000$.   $47.5 \div 1000 = 0.0475$ km
③Answer: $0.0475$ km
Tip: do conversions one step at a time along the chain above. Trying to jump two steps at once is where most errors creep in.
3 Area and Volume Units — the Big Trap
$1\text{ m}^2$ is NOT $100\text{ cm}^2$. A square metre is a square of side $100$ cm, so its area is $100 \times 100 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2$.
The rule: if the length conversion factor is $n$, then
• the area factor is $n^2$
• the volume factor is $n^3$
ConversionLengthAreaVolume
cm ↔ mm$\times 10$$\times 100$$\times 1000$
m ↔ cm$\times 100$$\times 10\,000$$\times 1\,000\,000$
km ↔ m$\times 1000$$\times 1\,000\,000$$\times 10^9$
Worked Example 3 — Converting an area

A carpet has area $6.4\text{ m}^2$. Convert this to $\text{cm}^2$.

①Length factor m → cm is $\times 100$.
②Area factor is $100^2 = 10\,000$.
③$6.4 \times 10\,000 = 64\,000$
④Answer: $64\,000\text{ cm}^2$
Worked Example 4 — Converting a volume

A tank holds $2\,500\,000\text{ cm}^3$ of water. Convert this to $\text{m}^3$ and then to litres.

①Volume factor cm³ → m³ is $\div 1\,000\,000$.
②$2\,500\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 2.5\text{ m}^3$
③$1\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ ml, so $2\,500\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 2\,500\,000$ ml.
④$2\,500\,000 \div 1000 = 2500$ litres.

Answer: $2.5\text{ m}^3 = 2500$ litres

4 Units of Time

Time is the one measure that is not metric, so be extra careful.

FromToDo this
hoursminutes$\times 60$
minutesseconds$\times 60$
hoursseconds$\times 3600$
$2$ hours $30$ minutes is $2.5$ hours, not $2.3$ hours.
To turn minutes into a decimal of an hour, divide by $60$:   $30 \div 60 = 0.5$.
Worked Example 5 — Minutes to decimal hours

Write $3$ hours $24$ minutes as a decimal number of hours.

①$24$ minutes $= 24 \div 60 = 0.4$ hours
②$3 + 0.4 = 3.4$ hours

And in reverse: $1.75$ hours $= 0.75 \times 60 = 45$ min, so $1$ h $45$ min.

5 Compound Units

A compound unit is made from two different measures combined, almost always by dividing one by the other. The word "per" (or the slash "/") tells you a division is happening.

QuantityFormulaTypical units
Speed$\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$m/s, km/h, mph
Density$\text{density} = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$g/cm³, kg/m³
Pressure$\text{pressure} = \dfrac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$N/m² (pascals), N/cm²
Rate of pay$\dfrac{\text{money}}{\text{time}}$£/hour
Unit price$\dfrac{\text{cost}}{\text{quantity}}$p/gram, £/litre
Fuel economy$\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{fuel}}$miles per gallon, km/litre
Population density$\dfrac{\text{people}}{\text{area}}$people/km²
Reading a compound unit
"km/h" literally means "kilometres divided by hours".
The unit itself tells you the formula.
Worked Example 6 — Using a compound unit

A block of oak has mass $840$ g and volume $1200\text{ cm}^3$. Find its density.

①The unit g/cm³ says: mass $\div$ volume.
②$840 \div 1200 = 0.7$
③Density $= 0.7\text{ g/cm}^3$
6 Converting Between Compound Units

To convert a compound unit you must convert both parts. Do it one part at a time.

  • Write the quantity as a fraction, e.g. $\dfrac{72\text{ km}}{1\text{ h}}$.
  • Convert the top into the unit you want.
  • Convert the bottom into the unit you want.
  • Divide to get the new value.
Worked Example 7 — km/h into m/s

Convert $72$ km/h into m/s.

①$72$ km/h means $72$ km travelled in $1$ hour.
②Top: $72$ km $= 72 \times 1000 = 72\,000$ m
③Bottom: $1$ hour $= 3600$ s
④$\dfrac{72\,000}{3600} = 20$

Answer: $20$ m/s

Shortcut: km/h $\to$ m/s, divide by $3.6$.   m/s $\to$ km/h, multiply by $3.6$.   ($72 \div 3.6 = 20$ ✓)
Worked Example 8 — g/cm³ into kg/m³

The density of aluminium is $2.7\text{ g/cm}^3$. Convert this to $\text{kg/m}^3$.

①Write it as $\dfrac{2.7\text{ g}}{1\text{ cm}^3}$.
②Top: $2.7$ g $= 2.7 \div 1000 = 0.0027$ kg
③Bottom: $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.000001\text{ m}^3$
④$\dfrac{0.0027}{0.000001} = 2700$

Answer: $2700\text{ kg/m}^3$

In other words, to go from g/cm³ to kg/m³ you simply multiply by $1000$.

Worked Example 9 — Miles and kilometres

A road sign says the next town is $56$ km away. Roughly how many miles is this? Use $5$ miles $= 8$ km.

①How many lots of $8$ km?  $56 \div 8 = 7$
②Each lot is $5$ miles: $7 \times 5 = 35$

Answer: $35$ miles

Sense check: miles are longer than km, so the number of miles should be smaller ✓

Worked Example 10 — Mixing conversions with money

Petrol in the UK costs £$1.48$ per litre. In the USA it costs $$3.60$ per US gallon. Given $1$ US gallon $= 3.79$ litres and £$1$ = $$1.25$, which country's petrol is cheaper?

①US price per litre in dollars: $3.60 \div 3.79 = 0.9499\ldots$, i.e. $$0.95$
②Convert to pounds: $0.9499 \div 1.25 = £0.7599\ldots$
③Compare: UK £$1.48$/litre vs USA £$0.76$/litre.

USA petrol is cheaper — about half the UK price.

8 Quick Reference

Direction rule

Smaller unit → bigger number → multiply. Bigger unit → smaller number → divide.

Length

mm $\to$ cm $\div10$, cm $\to$ m $\div100$, m $\to$ km $\div1000$.

Area & volume

Square the length factor for area, cube it for volume.

Capacity link

$1\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ ml, $1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre, $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000$ litres.

Time

Minutes $\to$ decimal hours: $\div 60$. Never write $2$ h $30$ as $2.3$.

Speed shortcut

km/h $\div 3.6 =$ m/s.   m/s $\times 3.6 =$ km/h.

Density shortcut

g/cm³ $\times 1000 =$ kg/m³.

Imperial

$5$ miles $= 8$ km, $1$ kg $= 2.2$ lb, $1$ gallon $= 4.5$ litres.

9 Practice Questions
Question 1

Convert: (a) $2.7$ km to metres   (b) $845$ g to kilograms   (c) $0.36$ litres to millilitres.

▶ Show solution

(a) km → m is $\times 1000$:  $2.7 \times 1000 = 2700$ m

(b) g → kg is $\div 1000$:  $845 \div 1000 = 0.845$ kg

(c) litres → ml is $\times 1000$:  $0.36 \times 1000 = 360$ ml

Question 2

A rectangle measures $2.4$ m by $150$ cm. Find its area in $\text{m}^2$.

▶ Show solution

Make the units match first. $150$ cm $= 150 \div 100 = 1.5$ m.

Area $= 2.4 \times 1.5 = 3.6$

Answer: $3.6\text{ m}^2$

Question 3

Convert $0.045\text{ m}^2$ into $\text{cm}^2$.

▶ Show solution

Length factor m → cm is $\times 100$, so the area factor is $100^2 = 10\,000$.

$0.045 \times 10\,000 = 450$

Answer: $450\text{ cm}^2$

Question 4

A fish tank is a cuboid measuring $80$ cm by $40$ cm by $50$ cm. How many litres of water does it hold when full?

▶ Show solution

Volume $= 80 \times 40 \times 50 = 160\,000\text{ cm}^3$

$1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre, so divide by $1000$:

$160\,000 \div 1000 = 160$

Answer: $160$ litres

Question 5

Write (a) $4$ hours $12$ minutes as a decimal number of hours, and (b) $2.85$ hours in hours and minutes.

▶ Show solution

(a) $12 \div 60 = 0.2$, so the time is $4.2$ hours.

(b) $0.85 \times 60 = 51$ minutes, so $2.85$ h $= 2$ hours $51$ minutes.

Question 6

A cyclist travels at $9$ m/s. Convert this speed to km/h.

▶ Show solution

In $1$ second: $9$ m. In $1$ hour ($3600$ s): $9 \times 3600 = 32\,400$ m.

$32\,400$ m $= 32\,400 \div 1000 = 32.4$ km.

Answer: $32.4$ km/h  (check: $9 \times 3.6 = 32.4$ ✓)

Question 7

Mercury has a density of $13\,600\text{ kg/m}^3$. Convert this into $\text{g/cm}^3$.

▶ Show solution

kg/m³ → g/cm³ is $\div 1000$ (the reverse of the shortcut in Section 6).

$13\,600 \div 1000 = 13.6$

Answer: $13.6\text{ g/cm}^3$

Full working: $13\,600$ kg $= 13\,600\,000$ g; $1\text{ m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$; $13\,600\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 13.6$ ✓

Question 8

A plumber charges £$34$ per hour. She works from $08{:}45$ to $14{:}15$. How much does she charge?

▶ Show solution

Time worked: $08{:}45 \to 14{:}15$ is $5$ hours $30$ minutes.

As a decimal: $30 \div 60 = 0.5$, so $5.5$ hours.

Charge $= 34 \times 5.5 = £187$

Question 9

A car's fuel tank holds $12$ gallons. Using $1$ gallon $= 4.5$ litres, and given that fuel costs £$1.52$ per litre, find the cost of filling the tank from empty.

▶ Show solution

Capacity in litres: $12 \times 4.5 = 54$ litres.

Cost $= 54 \times 1.52 = £82.08$

Question 10

A goods lorry may not exceed $7.5$ tonnes. It weighs $4200$ kg when empty and is loaded with $180$ boxes each of mass $16.5$ kg. Is the lorry within the limit?

▶ Show solution

Mass of boxes $= 180 \times 16.5 = 2970$ kg.

Total mass $= 4200 + 2970 = 7170$ kg.

Limit $= 7.5$ tonnes $= 7.5 \times 1000 = 7500$ kg.

$7170 < 7500$, so yes, the lorry is within the limit — with $330$ kg to spare.
